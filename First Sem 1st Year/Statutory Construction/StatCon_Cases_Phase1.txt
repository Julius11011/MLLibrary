1. Caltex v. Palomar, G.R. No. L-19650, 29 September 1966
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2) Who is the plaintiff: Caltex (Philippines), Inc. (Petitioner)
3) Where the case file: G.R. No. L-19650, September 29, 1966 (Supreme Court)
4) Who is the complainant/ peace officer/public officer: Caltex (Philippines), Inc. (represented by its legal counsel)
5) Where MTC/MCTC the case prosecuted or what court where the case is filed: Court of First Instance (CFI) of Manila (Branch XIII)
6) Who is the prosecutor execute the case trial: Respondent represented by the Solicitor General (Antonio P. Barredo)

a. Whole Facts:
Caltex Philippines conceived a promotional campaign called the "Caltex Hooded Pump Contest." The contest was open to all motor vehicle owners or licensed drivers. Participants did not have to purchase any Caltex products or pay any fee to join; they only needed to fill out an entry form at any Caltex station. The winners would be determined by a drawing of lots, and the prizes consisted of motor vehicles. Caltex sought clearance from the Postmaster General, Enrico Palomar, to mail the contest rules and entry forms. Palomar denied the request, ruling that the contest violated the Postal Law (Section 1954-A of the Revised Administrative Code), which prohibits the mailing of lottery or gift enterprise materials. Palomar threatened to reject all Caltex mailings if they proceeded. Caltex filed a petition for declaratory relief.

a.1. Explanation of the Facts:
Caltex wanted to run a marketing contest where people could win cars by drawing lots without buying anything. The Postmaster General blocked them, saying it was a "lottery" or "gift enterprise" prohibited from using the postal system. This created a legal conflict over the definition of these statutory terms, prompting Caltex to sue to have the court interpret the law.

b. The Issue of the Case:
Whether the "Caltex Hooded Pump Contest" constitutes a "lottery" or "gift enterprise" under Section 1954-A of the Revised Administrative Code, thereby prohibiting the mailing of its promotional materials.

b.1. Explanation of the Issue:
The court had to decide if the contest fit the legal definition of a lottery. If it did, Palomar's ban was valid; if it did not, Paltex was free to use the mails. The issue centered on interpreting the exact statutory elements of a "lottery" and a "gift enterprise" when applied to a modern marketing scheme.

c. Case Violation in Statutory Construction:
The Postmaster General committed a violation by attempting to expand the statutory definition of "lottery" and "gift enterprise" beyond their plain and established legal meanings. By attempting to penalize a scheme that did not require consideration (payment), Palomar was effectively rewriting the law to insert an unwritten restriction, which constitutes judicial/administrative legislation rather than construction.

d. Court Ruling / Doctrine:
The Supreme Court ruled that the contest was NOT a lottery or a gift enterprise. 
* A "lottery" requires three essential elements: (1) consideration (payment), (2) chance, and (3) a prize. Since participants did not have to buy anything or pay a fee to join, the element of consideration was absent.
* A "gift enterprise" likewise requires that the distribution of prizes be coupled with the purchase of a product or service. 
Therefore, the contest was legal, and the postal ban was invalid.

d.1. Explanation of Doctrinal Execution:
This case is a classic execution of the court's power to interpret statutory terms strictly according to their established legal components. The Supreme Court demonstrated that executive officers cannot expand the scope of prohibitory laws through administrative interpretation. It reinforced the rule that where the law does not define a term, it must be understood in its technical legal sense, preventing administrative overreach.

Applicable Study Guide Topic:
* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - B. Statutory Construction
* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - F. Ambiguity / Vagueness
* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - G. Statutory Construction v. Judicial Legislation
* Correlation Explanation: This case serves as a fundamental introduction to the study of statutory construction because it defines what construction is and explicitly prevents judicial or administrative legislation. It applies because the court had to resolve an ambiguity (whether the terms "lottery" and "gift enterprise" include free marketing campaigns), but ruled that administrative officials cannot expand the law to include activities that lack the essential element of consideration (payment), as that would amount to rewriting the statute.

2. Tañada v. Tuvera, G.R. No. L-63915, 29 December 1986
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2) Who is the plaintiff: Lorenzo M. Tañada, Renato Constantino, Jose W. Diokno, et al. (Petitioners)
3) Where the case file: G.R. No. L-63915, December 29, 1986 (Supreme Court)
4) Who is the complainant/ peace officer/public officer: Lorenzo M. Tañada and other public-spirited citizens and lawyers
5) Where MTC/MCTC the case prosecuted or what court where the case is filed: Supreme Court of the Philippines (directly filed as a petition for mandamus)
6) Who is the prosecutor execute the case trial: Petitioners prosecuted the mandamus case directly; Respondents represented by the Solicitor General

a. Whole Facts:
During the martial law era under President Ferdinand Marcos, numerous presidential decrees, executive orders, letters of instructions, and administrative rules were issued. Many of these decrees were never published in the Official Gazette, yet the government enforced them as active laws. Lorenzo Tañada and other civic-minded citizens filed a petition for mandamus to compel the Executive Secretary, Juan C. Tuvera, to publish these decrees in the Official Gazette. The respondents argued that publication was not mandatory when the law itself provided for its own date of effectivity (e.g., "This decree shall take effect immediately upon approval").

a.1. Explanation of the Facts:
The government was enacting and enforcing secret laws under the guise of presidential decrees without showing them to the public. Citizens sued to force the government to print these laws, arguing that people cannot obey laws they do not know exist. The government claimed that if a decree said "effective immediately," it bypassed the publication requirement.

b. The Issue of the Case:
Whether publication in the Official Gazette is a mandatory requirement for the effectivity of laws, presidential decrees, and administrative rules, even if such laws declare they shall take effect "immediately."

b.1. Explanation of the Issue:
The court had to interpret Article 2 of the Civil Code: "Laws shall take effect after fifteen days following the completion of their publication in the Official Gazette, unless it is otherwise provided." The issue was whether the phrase "unless it is otherwise provided" allowed the legislature to skip publication entirely, or if it only allowed them to change the 15-day period.

c. Case Violation in Statutory Construction:
The executive branch violated the fundamental rule of constitutional and statutory construction by reading the clause "unless it is otherwise provided" as an excuse to completely bypass the publication requirement. This interpretation violated the due process clause of the Constitution and the spirit of the Civil Code.

d. Court Ruling / Doctrine:
The Supreme Court ruled that publication is MANDATORY. The clause "unless it is otherwise provided" refers to the *date* of effectivity (e.g., making it longer or shorter than 15 days, or making it immediate), but it does NOT excuse the requirement of publication itself. All statutes, presidential decrees, administrative rules of general applicability, and charter laws must be published in full in the Official Gazette (or, per subsequent laws, in a newspaper of general circulation) as a condition for their effectivity. Unpublished laws have no legal force.

d.1. Explanation of Doctrinal Execution:
This doctrine is a cornerstone of the rule of law and due process. The Supreme Court executed its power of interpretation to protect citizens from secret legislation. By separating the "fact of publication" (which is absolute) from the "timing of effectivity" (which can be varied), the court harmonized the statute with the constitutional requirement of due process, establishing that "ignorantia legis non excusat" (ignorance of the law excuses no one) can only apply if the law is made publicly accessible.

Applicable Study Guide Topic:
* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - A. Statutes
* II. PRELUDE TO THE EXERCISE OF THE POWER - A. Verba Legis Non Est Recedendum
* II. PRELUDE TO THE EXERCISE OF THE POWER - B. Absoluta Sententia Expositore Non Indigent
* Correlation Explanation: This case is directly correlated to "A. Statutes" because it outlines the constitutional and statutory prerequisites for a law's validity—specifically, the absolute requirement of public notice. It is also applicable to the *Verba Legis* and *Absoluta Sententia* doctrines because the court literally interpreted Article 2 of the Civil Code, holding that the phrase "unless it is otherwise provided" is clear in its limitation: it can only modify the transition period of effectivity, but cannot be read to completely erase the publication mandate.

3. Romualdez v. Sandiganbayan, G.R. No. 152259, July 29, 2004
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2) Who is the plaintiff: People of the Philippines (represented by the Special Prosecutor)
3) Where the case file: G.R. No. 152259, July 29, 2004 (Supreme Court)
4) Who is the complainant/ peace officer/public officer: Presidential Commission on Good Government (PCGG) / Ombudsman Investigators
5) Where MTC/MCTC the case prosecuted or what court where the case is filed: Sandiganbayan (First Division)
6) Who is the prosecutor execute the case trial: Office of the Special Prosecutor (OSP) under the Office of the Ombudsman

a. Whole Facts:
Alfredo Romualdez, a relative of former First Lady Imelda Marcos, was charged with violating Section 5 of Republic Act No. 3019 (The Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act) for allegedly intervening in a government contract with the National Power Corporation. Romualdez moved to quash the information, arguing that Section 5 was unconstitutionally vague because it did not define the terms "intervene" or "family relation." The Sandiganbayan denied the motion. Romualdez petitioned the Supreme Court, asserting a facial challenge against the graft law on the grounds of vagueness.

a.1. Explanation of the Facts:
A relative of Imelda Marcos was prosecuted for corruption because he allegedly intervened in a government deal. He tried to get the case thrown out by arguing that the anti-graft law was so poorly written and vague that no one could tell what "intervene" or "family relation" meant, making the law unconstitutional.

b. The Issue of the Case:
Whether Section 5 of R.A. No. 3019 is unconstitutionally vague, and whether a facial challenge based on the vagueness doctrine can be used to strike down a penal statute.

b.1. Explanation of the Issue:
The court had to determine if the graft law violated due process by failing to give fair warning of what conduct was prohibited. It also had to decide if a criminal defendant could challenge a penal law "on its face" (meaning the law is invalid in all its applications) rather than "as applied" to his specific conduct.

c. Case Violation in Statutory Construction:
Romualdez attempted to misapply the "vagueness doctrine" by using a facial challenge—a statutory construction tool reserved primarily for First Amendment/free speech cases—to strike down a penal statute.

d. Court Ruling / Doctrine:
The Supreme Court ruled that Section 5 is constitutional. 
* A facial challenge on the ground of vagueness is not applicable to penal statutes; it can only be raised in "as-applied" cases, except in free speech cases.
* The words "intervene" and "family relation" are not vague when read in their common and statutory context. "Intervene" means to consciously introduce oneself into a transaction, and "family relation" is defined within the civil law context. 
The motion to quash was denied.

d.1. Explanation of Doctrinal Execution:
The Supreme Court executed a strict limit on constitutional construction. It established that courts must presume statutes are constitutional and should not strike down penal laws on facial vagueness if they can be reasonably interpreted in context. This prevents defendants from using semantic arguments to escape prosecution, preserving the legislature's intent to penalize graft.

Applicable Study Guide Topic:
* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - F. Ambiguity / Vagueness
* VI. CONSTRUCTION OF CONSTITUTION AND SPECIFIC KINDS OF STATUTES - B. Statutes Strictly Construed
* Correlation Explanation: This case is applicable to "F. Ambiguity / Vagueness" because it defines the limits of the vagueness doctrine, establishing that a law is not unconstitutionally vague simply because it contains terms that require judicial interpretation. It applies to "Statutes Strictly Construed" because it shows that penal laws and their written scales must be applied literally as written, even if economic inflation has made the punishment disproportionate over time.

4. Pesca v. Pesca, G.R. No. 136921, 17 April 2001
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2) Who is the plaintiff: Lorna G. Pesca (Petitioner/Plaintiff)
3) Where the case file: G.R. No. 136921, April 17, 2001 (Supreme Court)
4) Who is the complainant/ peace officer/public officer: Lorna G. Pesca (wife who initiated the civil annulment case)
5) Where MTC/MCTC the case prosecuted or what court where the case is filed: Regional Trial Court (RTC) of Pasig City (Branch 160)
6) Who is the prosecutor execute the case trial: Private counsel of Lorna Pesca (civil trial); State represented by Public Prosecutor to prevent collusion

a. Whole Facts:
Lorna Pesca filed a petition for the declaration of nullity of her marriage to her husband, Lorimer Pesca, on the ground of psychological incapacity under Article 36 of the Family Code. She presented evidence of physical abuse and domestic violence. The trial court declared the marriage void. The Court of Appeals reversed, citing the strict guidelines established in the Supreme Court cases of *Santos v. Court of Appeals* and *Republic v. Molina*. Lorna petitioned the Supreme Court, arguing that the *Santos* and *Molina* guidelines should not apply retroactively to her case, as her petition was filed before those decisions were promulgated.

a.1. Explanation of the Facts:
Lorna sued to annul her marriage because of her husband's physical abuse, claiming it proved "psychological incapacity." While her case was ongoing, the Supreme Court laid down very strict guidelines in other cases (*Santos* and *Molina*) defining psychological incapacity. Lorna argued those guidelines should not be applied to her case because she filed her lawsuit before those guidelines were written.

b. The Issue of the Case:
Whether the guidelines on psychological incapacity laid down in the *Santos* and *Molina* cases should be applied retroactively to pending cases.

b.1. Explanation of the Issue:
The court had to interpret the nature of judicial decisions under statutory construction. Specifically, whether judicial interpretations of a statute take effect only from the date of the decision (prospective) or if they apply to all cases covered by the statute from its enactment (retroactive).

c. Case Violation in Statutory Construction:
Lorna violated the rule on the nature of judicial interpretations by arguing that Supreme Court decisions are equivalent to new statutes, which are generally prospective.

d. Court Ruling / Doctrine:
The Supreme Court ruled that judicial decisions interpreting a law form part of that law from the date of its enactment. The guidelines in *Santos* and *Molina* do not constitute new laws but are merely interpretations of what Article 36 of the Family Code has always meant since its approval in 1988. Therefore, they apply retroactively to Lorna's case. Physical abuse, while a ground for legal separation, does not automatically constitute psychological incapacity under the interpreted guidelines. The petition was denied.

d.1. Explanation of Doctrinal Execution:
The Supreme Court strongly executed the doctrine that judicial interpretations are retroactive. Unlike legislative acts which create new rules and are prospective, the judiciary's role is to declare what the law has always meant. This reinforces the court's role as the supreme expositor of statutory meaning, ensuring consistent application of the law across all pending and future disputes.

Applicable Study Guide Topic:
* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - H. Legis Interpretado Legis Vim Obtinet
* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - I. Kinds of Construction
* Correlation Explanation: This case is the leading authority on the maxim *Legis interpretado legis vim obtinet* (interpretation of the law has the force of law) because it holds that Supreme Court decisions are incorporated into the law itself. It directly correlates to the prospectivity/retroactivity of construction, clarifying that judicial interpretations must apply retroactively to pending disputes because the court is not creating a new law but merely revealing the statute's original, true meaning.

5. People v. Mapa, G.R. No. L-22301, 30 August 1967
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2) Who is the plaintiff: People of the Philippines (Plaintiff-Appellee)
3) Where the case file: G.R. No. L-22301, August 30, 1967 (Supreme Court)
4) Who is the complainant/ peace officer/public officer: Police officers of the Batangas Police Department / Governor's office
5) Where MTC/MCTC the case prosecuted or what court where the case is filed: Court of First Instance (CFI) of Batangas
6) Who is the prosecutor execute the case trial: Provincial Fiscal (Prosecutor) of Batangas

a. Whole Facts:
Jose Mapa was charged with illegal possession of unlicensed firearm (a home-made revolver). Mapa admitted possessing the firearm but presented as his defense that he was appointed as a secret agent by the Governor of Batangas. He argued that as a secret agent, he was an extension of the peace officers of the province and was therefore exempt from the license requirement under Section 879 of the Revised Administrative Code, which exempts "peace officers."

a.1. Explanation of the Facts:
Jose Mapa was caught with an unlicensed gun. He argued he didn't need a license because he was hired as a secret agent for the governor, which he claimed made him a "peace officer" exempt from gun laws. The prosecution argued that "secret agents" were not listed in the law's exemptions.

b. The Issue of the Case:
Whether a "secret agent" appointed by a provincial governor is included in the definition of "peace officer" exempt from the firearm licensing requirements under Section 879 of the Revised Administrative Code.

b.1. Explanation of the Issue:
The issue was whether the court could expand the plain language of the gun exemption statute to include "secret agents," which were not explicitly mentioned in the text but arguably performed similar duties to peace officers.

c. Case Violation in Statutory Construction:
The defendant wanted the court to commit a violation of the plain meaning rule by reading an unwritten exemption ("secret agent") into a highly specific penal statute, which would amount to judicial legislation.

d. Court Ruling / Doctrine:
The Supreme Court ruled that Jose Mapa was GUILTY. The law is clear and explicit: it exempts peace officers, and it does not mention secret agents. When the law is clear, there is no room for interpretation—only application. Courts cannot write into the law an exemption that the legislature omitted. Mapa's conviction was affirmed.

d.1. Explanation of Doctrinal Execution:
This case is a classic example of the *Verba Legis* doctrine. The Supreme Court executed its power by refusing to act as a legislature. It established that in criminal statutes, exemptions must be construed strictly. The court's ruling sends a strong message that it will strictly enforce the literal words of the law to prevent individuals from creating unauthorized legal defenses through administrative appointments.

Applicable Study Guide Topic:
* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - J. Extent of the Power to Construe
* II. PRELUDE TO THE EXERCISE OF THE POWER - A. Verba Legis Non Est Recedendum
* Correlation Explanation: This case is the absolute textbook illustration of *Verba Legis Non Est Recedendum* (from the words of the law there must be no departure). It is applicable because it strictly limits the "Extent of the Power to Construe," establishing that courts have zero authority to interpret, expand, or add words to a statute when the text is already clear and unambiguous, even if a strict application leads to a conviction.

6. Endencia v. David, G.R. No. L-6355-56, 31 August 1953
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2) Who is the plaintiff: Pastor M. Endencia and Sebastian T. Lopez (Plaintiffs-Appellees)
3) Where the case file: G.R. No. L-6355-56, August 31, 1953 (Supreme Court)
4) Who is the complainant/ peace officer/public officer: Saturnino David (Collector of Internal Revenue who initiated tax assessments)
5) Where MTC/MCTC the case prosecuted or what court where the case is filed: Court of First Instance (CFI) of Manila
6) Who is the prosecutor execute the case trial: Solicitor General representing the Collector of Internal Revenue

a. Whole Facts:
In the case of *Perfecto v. Meer*, the Supreme Court ruled that taxing the salaries of judicial officers (judges and justices) was unconstitutional because it amounted to a decrease in their compensation, violating Article VIII, Section 9 of the 1935 Constitution. In response, Congress enacted Republic Act No. 590, which contained Section 13, explicitly declaring that the taxing of judicial salaries shall not be construed as a diminution of their compensation. The Collector of Internal Revenue, Saturnino David, proceeded to assess taxes on the salaries of Justices Pastor Endencia and others. The Justices challenged the constitutionality of Section 13.

a.1. Explanation of the Facts:
The Supreme Court previously ruled that taxing judges' salaries was unconstitutional. Congress got angry and passed a new law declaring that taxing judges' salaries is NOT unconstitutional. The tax collector then tried to tax the judges. The judges sued, arguing Congress cannot overrule a Supreme Court interpretation of the Constitution by passing a declaratory law.

b. The Issue of the Case:
Whether Section 13 of R.A. No. 590, which interprets the constitutional provision on judicial compensation, is constitutional.

b.1. Explanation of the Issue:
The core issue was which branch has the ultimate authority to interpret the Constitution and statutes. Can Congress pass a law defining how a constitutional provision must be construed, thereby overriding a prior Supreme Court interpretation?

c. Case Violation in Statutory Construction:
Congress violated the principle of separation of powers by attempting to perform a judicial function. By passing a statute that declared how the Constitution should be interpreted, Congress engaged in "legislative interpretation," which encroached upon the exclusive situs of statutory and constitutional construction.

d. Court Ruling / Doctrine:
The Supreme Court ruled that Section 13 of R.A. No. 590 is UNCONSTITUTIONAL. The interpretation of the Constitution and statutes is an exclusive judicial function. Congress has the power to enact laws, but once a law is enacted, the power to interpret it and determine its constitutionality belongs solely to the courts. Congress cannot overrule judicial construction through legislative declaration.

d.1. Explanation of Doctrinal Execution:
This is a defining case for the independence of the judiciary. The Supreme Court strongly executed the doctrine of judicial supremacy in interpretation. It established that the legislature cannot dictate to the courts how to construe laws or the Constitution. This prevents the legislature from bypassing constitutional checks and balances through declaratory statutes.

Applicable Study Guide Topic:
* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - C. Situs of Construction
* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - G. Statutory Construction v. Judicial Legislation
* Correlation Explanation: This case directly defines the "Situs of Construction," establishing that the power to interpret the Constitution and laws belongs exclusively to the judicial department. It violates "Statutory Construction v. Judicial Legislation" because it invalidates Congress's attempt to engage in legislative interpretation, ruling that the legislature cannot dictate statutory meaning to the courts or overrule judicial rulings through declaratory statutes.

7. People v. Corpuz, G.R. No. 180016, 29 April 2014
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2) Who is the plaintiff: People of the Philippines (Plaintiff-Appellee)
3) Where the case file: G.R. No. 180016, April 29, 2014 (Supreme Court)
4) Who is the complainant/ peace officer/public officer: Lialinda Macoto (private complainant/victim of Estafa)
5) Where MTC/MCTC the case prosecuted or what court where the case is filed: Regional Trial Court (RTC) of Manila (Branch 46)
6) Who is the prosecutor execute the case trial: Public Prosecutor of Manila assisted by private prosecutor

a. Whole Facts:
Danilo Corpuz was charged with Estafa under Article 315 of the Revised Penal Code. He received pieces of jewelry from Lialinda Macoto to sell on commission, but failed to return the jewelry or remit the proceeds. The value of the jewelry was P98,000. Corpuz was convicted, and the penalty imposed was a high prison term based on the penalty scale written in the Revised Penal Code of 1932, which calculated penalties based on the value of the property stolen. Corpuz appealed, arguing that the penalty was unconstitutional as it was cruel and unusual, given that P98,000 in 1932 had a vastly different purchasing power than in 2014.

a.1. Explanation of the Facts:
Corpuz stole jewelry worth P98,000. Under the old 1932 penal law, because P98,000 was a massive sum back then, he was sentenced to a very long prison term (up to 15 years). He argued that the court should adjust the penalty scale to account for inflation, as P98,000 today is worth much less, making his long sentence unfair.

b. The Issue of the Case:
Whether the courts can adjust the penalties written in a penal statute (Article 315 of the RPC) to account for inflation and the changed value of money over time.

b.1. Explanation of the Issue:
The court had to decide if it could apply "progressive interpretation" or equity to modify the penalty scales of a penal law, or if it was bound to apply the literal terms written in 1932 until Congress amended the law.

c. Case Violation in Statutory Construction:
The appellant urged the court to engage in judicial legislation by rewriting the penalty thresholds of the Revised Penal Code, which would violate the rule of separation of powers and the *Verba Legis* doctrine.

d. Court Ruling / Doctrine:
The Supreme Court ruled that it CANNOT adjust the penalties. The law is clear as to the monetary thresholds, and courts cannot modify the law based on inflation. The power to define crimes and prescribe penalties belongs exclusively to the legislature. The court affirmed the conviction but strongly urged Congress to amend the outdated penalty scales (which Congress eventually did in 2017 via R.A. 10951).

d.1. Explanation of Doctrinal Execution:
The Supreme Court executed a strict self-restraint policy. It established that even if a penal law has become obsolete or harsh due to economic inflation, the court cannot step into the shoes of the legislature to rewrite it. This preserves the constitutional separation of powers, forcing the legislature to perform its duty while the court remains a strict applicator of the written text.

Applicable Study Guide Topic:
* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - G. Statutory Construction v. Judicial Legislation
* VI. CONSTRUCTION OF CONSTITUTION AND SPECIFIC KINDS OF STATUTES - B. Statutes Strictly Construed
* Correlation Explanation: This case is applicable to "G. Statutory Construction v. Judicial Legislation" because it enforces strict boundaries on judicial power, declaring that adjusting penal sentences for inflation is a legislative policy decision, and judicial adjustment would constitute illegal legislative encroachment. It applies to "Statutes Strictly Construed" because it shows that penal laws and their written scales must be applied literally as written, even if economic inflation has made the punishment disproportionate over time.

8. Republic v. Molina, G.R. No. 108763, 13 February 1997
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2) Who is the plaintiff: Roridel Olaviano Molina (Original Plaintiff) / Republic of the Philippines (Petitioner on Appeal)
3) Where the case file: G.R. No. 108763, February 13, 1997 (Supreme Court)
4) Who is the complainant/ peace officer/public officer: Roridel Olaviano Molina (wife who filed the petition)
5) Where MTC/MCTC the case prosecuted or what court where the case is filed: Regional Trial Court (RTC) of Pasig City (Branch 152)
6) Who is the prosecutor execute the case trial: Public Prosecutor of Pasig City / Office of the Solicitor General (OSG) representing the State

a. Whole Facts:
Roridel Molina filed a petition to declare her marriage to Reynaldo Molina void on the ground of psychological incapacity under Article 36 of the Family Code. Roridel claimed that Reynaldo was irresponsible, preferred to spend time with friends, failed to support the family, and was quick-tempered. The trial court and the Court of Appeals declared the marriage void. The Republic, through the Solicitor General, appealed, arguing that the husband's behavior did not constitute psychological incapacity but was merely a case of marital incompatibility.

a.1. Explanation of the Facts:
A wife wanted an annulment because her husband was lazy, irresponsible, and bad-tempered. The lower courts granted the annulment. The government appealed, arguing that if courts allow everyday bad behavior to qualify as "psychological incapacity," it would turn Article 36 into a default divorce law, which is not permitted under Philippine law.

b. The Issue of the Case:
What constitutes "psychological incapacity" under Article 36 of the Family Code, and what guidelines should courts follow in statutory construction to interpret this provision?

b.1. Explanation of the Issue:
Article 36 does not define "psychological incapacity." The issue was how the court should interpret this vague statutory term to prevent it from being abused as an easy divorce mechanism while still providing relief in genuine cases.

c. Case Violation in Statutory Construction:
The lower courts violated the legislative intent of Article 36 by construing "psychological incapacity" too liberally, expanding it to cover ordinary marital issues and incompatibility, which went against the constitutional policy protecting marriage as an inviolable social institution.

d. Court Ruling / Doctrine:
The Supreme Court reversed the annulment and laid down eight strict guidelines (the "Molina Guidelines") for interpreting Article 36:
1. The burden of proof belongs to the plaintiff.
2. The root cause of the incapacity must be medically or clinically identified.
3. The incapacity must be proven to be existing at the time of the celebration of the marriage.
4. The incapacity must be clinically or medically permanent or incurable.
5. The illness must be grave enough to prevent compliance with essential marital obligations.
6. The non-complied marital obligations must be essential.
7. The interpretations of the National Appellate Matrimonial Tribunal of the Catholic Church should be given great respect.
8. The Solicitor General must appear as a defender of the marital bond.

d.1. Explanation of Doctrinal Execution:
The Supreme Court executed its power to issue judicial guidelines to clarify a vague statute. Since the legislature left the term undefined, the court stepped in to provide a structured, binding interpretation. This ensured the law is applied uniformly across the country, preventing the creation of "judicial divorce" while keeping the law functional.

Applicable Study Guide Topic:
* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - E. When to Construe?
* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - J. Extent of the Power to Construe
* Correlation Explanation: This case applies to "E. When to Construe?" because the statutory term "psychological incapacity" is inherently ambiguous and undefined, representing a clear trigger for judicial construction. It correlates to the "Extent of the Power to Construe" because the Supreme Court went beyond simple word definition, executing its power to issue a binding, multi-part test (the Molina Guidelines) to guide all lower courts in the application of the law.

9. Public Estates Authority v. Caoibes, Jr., G.R. No. 132426, 19 August 1999
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2) Who is the plaintiff: Public Estates Authority (Petitioner)
3) Where the case file: G.R. No. 132426, August 19, 1999 (Supreme Court)
4) Who is the complainant/ peace officer/public officer: Public Estates Authority (PEA) board and officers
5) Where MTC/MCTC the case prosecuted or what court where the case is filed: Regional Trial Court (RTC) of Imus, Cavite (Branch 20)
6) Who is the prosecutor execute the case trial: Legal counsel of PEA / Solicitor General

a. Whole Facts:
The Public Estates Authority (PEA) filed an expropriation (eminent domain) proceeding to acquire lands owned by respondents. During the pendency of the case, the parties entered into a compromise agreement. Later, PEA sought to dismiss the expropriation case, but the respondents objected, claiming they had already suffered damages. The trial court ruled in favor of the respondents, stating that PEA could not unilaterally withdraw from the expropriation case once the court had assumed jurisdiction, as the general Rules of Court governed the proceeding. a.1. Explanation of the Facts:
A government agency (PEA) tried to take over private land. While the court case was ongoing, PEA changed its mind and tried to drop the case. The landowners refused, saying they deserved damages for the disruption. The court agreed with the landowners, but PEA claimed its special charter gave it a unique right to walk away.

b. The Issue of the Case:
Whether the provisions of the Rules of Court (general law) on the dismissal of actions govern the expropriation proceedings of the PEA, or if the PEA's special charter (special law) grants it an absolute right to withdraw.

b.1. Explanation of the Issue:
The issue was one of statutory conflict: does a general procedural rule override a special charter grant, or does the special law take precedence as an exception to the general rule?

c. Case Violation in Statutory Construction:
The trial court violated the rule of *Generalia specialibus non derogant* by applying the general rules of civil procedure to defeat a specific power granted to an agency under its charter.

d. Court Ruling / Doctrine:
The Supreme Court ruled in favor of PEA. A special charter (special law) prevails over the Rules of Court (general law). The power of eminent domain is a special proceeding. PEA has the right to withdraw its expropriation complaint at any time before the entry of judgment, subject only to payment of just compensation for actual occupation of the property.

d.1. Explanation of Doctrinal Execution:
The Supreme Court executed the doctrine of special law supremacy. This maintains legislative intent and protects the administrative efficiency of government corporations.

19. Pascual v. Pascual-Bautista, G.R. No. 84240, March 25, 1992
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2) Who is the plaintiff: Olivia Pascual and Hermes Pascual (Petitioners)
3) Where the case file: G.R. No. 84240, March 25, 1992 (Supreme Court)
4) Who is the complainant/ peace officer/public officer: Olivia Pascual and Hermes Pascual (who initiated the intestate estate proceeding opposition)
5) Where MTC/MCTC the case prosecuted or what court where the case is filed: Regional Trial Court (RTC) of Malolos, Bulacan (Branch 13)
6) Who is the prosecutor execute the case trial: Private estate planning lawyers representing both factions of heirs

a. Whole Facts:
Olivia and Hermes Pascual were the illegitimate children of Ruperto Pascual. Ruperto was the brother of the deceased, intestate Olivia Pascual. When Olivia died without a will, the illegitimate children tried to inherit a portion of her estate by representing their deceased father, Ruperto. The legitimate heirs of Olivia opposed this, pointing to Article 992 of the Civil Code (the "iron curtain" rule), which prohibits illegitimate children from inheriting intestate from the legitimate relatives of their father or mother.

a.1. Explanation of the Facts:
Illegitimate children tried to inherit from their deceased aunt's estate. The aunt's legitimate family blocked them, citing a Civil Code rule that bans illegitimate children from inheriting from their parents' legitimate family. The illegitimate children argued this rule was discriminatory and should be interpreted liberally to promote family unity.

b. The Issue of the Case:
Whether Article 992 of the Civil Code should be interpreted to allow illegitimate children to inherit from the legitimate relatives of their parents by right of representation.

b.1. Explanation of the Issue:
The issue was whether the court could bypass the literal "iron curtain" rule of Article 992 based on modern ideas of equity and family fairness.

c. Case Violation in Statutory Construction:
The petitioners urged the court to breach the clear statutory barrier of Article 992 by using "equity" to create an unwritten exception, which would violate the *Verba Legis* rule.

d. Court Ruling / Doctrine:
The Supreme Court ruled that the illegitimate children CANNOT inherit. Article 992 is clear and absolute. It establishes a complete barrier between the legitimate and illegitimate families. The court must apply the law as written. If the law is harsh, the remedy is with Congress, not the courts.

d.1. Explanation of Doctrinal Execution:
The Supreme Court executed a strict *Verba Legis* and *Dura Lex Sed Lex* interpretation. It showed that in succession law, where rules are highly structured and specific, the court will not use general equity to break down statutory barriers. This maintains the integrity of the Civil Code's inheritance system.

Applicable Study Guide Topic:
* II. PRELUDE TO THE EXERCISE OF THE POWER - A. Verba Legis Non Est Recedendum
* Correlation Explanation: This case is applicable to *Verba Legis* and the maxim *Dura Lex Sed Lex*. It applies because the court literally enforced Article 992 of the Civil Code, refusing to lift the "iron curtain" separating the legitimate and illegitimate families. The court ruled that even if a law is harsh or seemingly out of touch with modern family concepts, the text is clear, and the court has no power to deviate from the literal wording of successional barriers.

20. Duncan v. Court of First Instance, G.R. No. L-30576, February 10, 1976
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2) Who is the plaintiff: Spouses Edwin Duncan and Velia Duncan (Petitioners)
3) Where the case file: G.R. No. L-30576, February 10, 1976 (Supreme Court)
4) Who is the complainant/ peace officer/public officer: Spouses Edwin and Velia Duncan (who filed adoption petition)
5) Where MTC/MCTC the case prosecuted or what court where the case is filed: Court of First Instance (CFI) of Rizal (Branch X)
6) Who is the prosecutor execute the case trial: Private family law counsel for Duncans / Assistant Provincial Fiscal of Rizal

a. Whole Facts:
A child named Colin was left with a couple, the Duncans, who raised him. Later, the Duncans filed a petition to adopt Colin. The natural mother of the child, who had initially abandoned him, later opposed the adoption and tried to reclaim the child. The trial court dismissed the adoption petition on a technical ground, stating that the consent of the natural mother had not been properly obtained under the strict literal requirements of the law. The Duncans appealed, arguing that the law should be interpreted liberally to promote the welfare of the child.

a.1. Explanation of the Facts:
A couple raised an abandoned child and tried to adopt him. The birth mother suddenly returned and blocked the adoption on a technicality. The court initially dismissed the adoption, but the adoptive parents asked the court to focus on the child's best interests rather than rigid legal technicalities.

b. The Issue of the Case:
How should the adoption laws be construed when there is a conflict between the literal technical consent requirements and the welfare of the child?

b.1. Explanation of the Issue:
The issue was whether adoption laws should be strictly construed against the adoption (to protect natural parental rights) or liberally construed (to protect the child's welfare).

c. Case Violation in Statutory Construction:
The trial court violated the rule of liberal construction of social and remedial statutes by prioritizing rigid technical consent rules over the child's best interests, which defeated the purpose of adoption laws.

d. Court Ruling / Doctrine:
The Supreme Court ruled in favor of the adoption. Adoption laws must be construed liberally in favor of the adoption and the best interests of the child. The welfare of the child is the supreme concern. Since the natural mother had abandoned the child for years, her consent was deemed waived.

d.1. Explanation of Doctrinal Execution:
The Supreme Court executed a purpose-driven, liberal interpretation of child welfare laws. It established that in cases involving the status and future of children, the court will brush aside technicalities to achieve the law's curative and humane objective: providing a stable family for an abandoned child.

Applicable Study Guide Topic:
* VI. CONSTRUCTION OF CONSTITUTION AND SPECIFIC KINDS OF STATUTES - C. Statutes Liberally Construed
* Correlation Explanation: This case is directly correlated to the "Liberal Construction of Social and Remedial Laws." It applies because adoption statutes are created to protect child welfare, and the court held that these laws must be construed with utmost liberality to prioritize the child's best interests over rigid, literal requirements of maternal consent when the child has been abandoned.

21. Alonzo v. Intermediate Appellate Court, G.R. No. 72873, May 28, 1987
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2) Who is the plaintiff: Carlos Alonzo and Casimira Alonzo (Petitioners)
3) Where the case file: G.R. No. 72873, May 28, 1987 (Supreme Court)
4) Who is the complainant/ peace officer/public officer: Semeona Padua (co-heir who initiated the redemption complaint)
5) Where MTC/MCTC the case prosecuted or what court where the case is filed: Court of First Instance (CFI) of Tarlac
6) Who is the prosecutor execute the case trial: Private civil litigators for the parties

a. Whole Facts:
Five siblings inherited a parcel of land. Two of the siblings sold their shares to spouses Alonzo in 1963 and 1964. The Alonzos built a house on the land and lived there openly. In 1977, one of the remaining siblings tried to redeem the sold shares, pointing to Article 1088 of the Civil Code, which grants co-heirs the right of redemption within 30 days from written notice of the sale. The sibling argued that since they were never given a *written* notice, the 30-day period had never started, meaning they could still redeem the land 13 years later.

a.1. Explanation of the Facts:
Two siblings sold their inherited land shares. The buyer moved in and built a house. Thirteen years later, another sibling tried to buy back the land, claiming that because the buyers never sent a formal "written letter" announcing the sale, the legal 30-day deadline to redeem the land had never started.

b. The Issue of the Case:
Whether the requirement of written notice under Article 1088 of the Civil Code is absolute, or if actual knowledge of the sale for 13 years satisfies the law's purpose, starting the 30-day redemption period.

b.1. Explanation of the Issue:
The issue was a conflict between the literal text ("written notice") and equity/common sense. Should the court allow a party to use the lack of a formal letter to undo a sale that they had actively witnessed and accepted for over a decade?

c. Case Violation in Statutory Construction:
The Intermediate Appellate Court committed a violation of equity by applying a literal interpretation that led to an absurd and unjust result (allowing redemption after 13 years of actual knowledge).

d. Court Ruling / Doctrine:
The Supreme Court reversed the decision and blocked the redemption. While the law literally requires "written notice," the purpose of the law is to ensure the co-heirs have actual knowledge of the sale. Because the siblings lived on the same land and saw the Alonzos build a house, they had actual knowledge. The court ruled that 13 years of actual knowledge is more than enough to satisfy the law. The 30-day period had long expired.

d.1. Explanation of Doctrinal Execution:
This is one of the most famous cases on equity overriding literalism. The Supreme Court executed its power to prevent the law from being used to perpetrate an injustice. The court ruled that "we must not allow the law to be used as a shield for bad faith." It established that actual notice can substitute for written notice when the passage of time makes a literal interpretation completely absurd.

Applicable Study Guide Topic:
* III. BASIC GUIDELINES IN THE CONSTRUCTION AND INTERPRETATION OF LAWS - A. GENERAL GUIDELINES - g. Construction to avoid absurdity and injustice
* Correlation Explanation: This is the definitive case on "Construction to Avoid Absurdity and Injustice." It applies because the court departed from the literal requirement of a "written notice," holding that a strict, literal reading after 13 years of actual knowledge would lead to a manifest absurdity and allow the law to be used as an instrument of bad faith, which goes against the basic guidelines of statutory construction.

22. Victoria v. Commission on Elections, G.R. No. 109005, January 10, 1994
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2) Who is the plaintiff: Francisco S. Victoria (Petitioner)
3) Where the case file: G.R. No. 109005, January 10, 1994 (Supreme Court)
4) Who is the complainant/ peace officer/public officer: Francisco S. Victoria (member of Sangguniang Panlalawigan)
5) Where MTC/MCTC the case prosecuted or what court where the case is filed: Commission on Elections (COMELEC)
6) Who is the prosecutor execute the case trial: Legal counsel of petitioner / COMELEC Legal Department

a. Whole Facts:
During the local elections, a vacancy occurred in the office of the Vice Governor of Catanduanes. Under the Local Government Code, the vacancy must be filled by the highest-ranking member of the Sangguniang Panlalawigan. A dispute arose between the candidates, Victoria and another, on who was the "highest-ranking" member. Victoria argued that ranking should be based on the number of votes obtained relative to the number of registered voters in their respective districts. The opponent argued it should be based strictly on the absolute number of votes received, as the text of the law simply mentioned "number of votes obtained."

a.1. Explanation of the Facts:
The vice governor's seat became vacant. The law stated the replacement must be the council member who got the "highest number of votes." Two council members fought over the seat: one got more absolute votes because their district was larger, while the other claimed they got a higher percentage of votes relative to their district's voter size.

b. The Issue of the Case:
How should the phrase "number of votes obtained" in the succession rules of the Local Government Code be construed?

b.1. Explanation of the Issue:
The issue was whether the court should apply the literal meaning of "number of votes" (absolute count) or if it should read in a mathematical formula (percentage of votes) to make the ranking "fairer" across different-sized districts.

c. Case Violation in Statutory Construction:
Victoria urged the court to rewrite the succession law by inserting a complex percentage calculation that was not written in the text, violating the *Verba Legis* rule.

d. Court Ruling / Doctrine:
The Supreme Court ruled that "number of votes obtained" means the absolute number of votes received. The law is clear and simple. Courts cannot introduce complex calculations to change a straightforward statutory term. The candidate with the highest absolute votes wins the succession.

d.1. Explanation of Doctrinal Execution:
The Supreme Court executed a strict *Verba Legis* interpretation. It showed that in political and administrative succession laws, the court will enforce the simplest literal meaning to ensure stability and prevent political disputes from dragging on in courts through debates over unwritten formulas.

Applicable Study Guide Topic:
* II. PRELUDE TO THE EXERCISE OF THE POWER - A. Verba Legis Non Est Recedendum
* Correlation Explanation: This case is applicable to *Verba Legis* because the court refused to deviate from the plain, literal meaning of "number of votes obtained." It holds that the court cannot read a complex percentage formula into an otherwise simple, absolute vote count requirement, enforcing the rule that where the text is clear, the court must apply it as written without inserting unwritten mathematical conditions.
