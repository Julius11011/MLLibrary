1. Caltex v. Palomar, G.R. No. L-19650, 29 September 1966

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

-------

2) Who is the plaintiff: Caltex (Philippines), Inc. (Petitioner)

3) Where the case file: G.R. No. L-19650, September 29, 1966 (Supreme

Court)

4) Who is the complainant/ peace officer/public officer: Caltex

(Philippines), Inc. (represented by its legal counsel)

5) Where MTC/MCTC the case prosecuted or what court where the case is

filed: Court of First Instance (CFI) of Manila (Branch XIII)

6) Who is the prosecutor execute the case trial: Respondent represented

by the Solicitor General (Antonio P. Barredo)

a. Whole Facts:

Caltex Philippines conceived a promotional campaign called the "Caltex

Hooded Pump Contest." The contest was open to all motor vehicle owners or

licensed drivers. Participants did not have to purchase any Caltex

products or pay any fee to join; they only needed to fill out an entry

form at any Caltex station. The winners would be determined by a drawing

of lots, and the prizes consisted of motor vehicles. Caltex sought

clearance from the Postmaster General, Enrico Palomar, to mail the

contest rules and entry forms. Palomar denied the request, ruling that

the contest violated the Postal Law (Section 1954-A of the Revised

Administrative Code), which prohibits the mailing of lottery or gift

enterprise materials. Palomar threatened to reject all Caltex mailings if

they proceeded. Caltex filed a petition for declaratory relief.

a.1. Explanation of the Facts:

Caltex wanted to run a marketing contest where people could win cars by

drawing lots without buying anything. The Postmaster General blocked

them, saying it was a "lottery" or "gift enterprise" prohibited from

using the postal system. This created a legal conflict over the

definition of these statutory terms, prompting Caltex to sue to have the

court interpret the law.

b. The Issue of the Case:

Whether the "Caltex Hooded Pump Contest" constitutes a "lottery" or "gift

enterprise" under Section 1954-A of the Revised Administrative Code,

thereby prohibiting the mailing of its promotional materials.

b.1. Explanation of the Issue:

The court had to decide if the contest fit the legal definition of a

lottery. If it did, Palomar's ban was valid; if it did not, Paltex was

free to use the mails. The issue centered on interpreting the exact

statutory elements of a "lottery" and a "gift enterprise" when applied to

a modern marketing scheme.

c. Case Violation in Statutory Construction:

The Postmaster General committed a violation by attempting to expand the

statutory definition of "lottery" and "gift enterprise" beyond their

plain and established legal meanings. By attempting to penalize a scheme

that did not require consideration (payment), Palomar was effectively

rewriting the law to insert an unwritten restriction, which constitutes

judicial/administrative legislation rather than construction.

d. Court Ruling / Doctrine:

The Supreme Court ruled that the contest was NOT a lottery or a gift

enterprise.

* A "lottery" requires three essential elements: (1) consideration

(payment), (2) chance, and (3) a prize. Since participants did not have

to buy anything or pay a fee to join, the element of consideration was

absent.

* A "gift enterprise" likewise requires that the distribution of prizes

be coupled with the purchase of a product or service.

Therefore, the contest was legal, and the postal ban was invalid.

d.1. Explanation of Doctrinal Execution:

This case is a classic execution of the court's power to interpret

statutory terms strictly according to their established legal components.

The Supreme Court demonstrated that executive officers cannot expand the

scope of prohibitory laws through administrative interpretation. It

reinforced the rule that where the law does not define a term, it must be

understood in its technical legal sense, preventing administrative

overreach.

Applicable Study Guide Topic:

* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - B. Statutory

Construction

* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - F. Ambiguity /

Vagueness

* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - G. Statutory

Construction v. Judicial Legislation

* Correlation Explanation: This case serves as a fundamental introduction

to the study of statutory construction because it defines what

construction is and explicitly prevents judicial or administrative

legislation. It applies because the court had to resolve an ambiguity

(whether the terms "lottery" and "gift enterprise" include free marketing

campaigns), but ruled that administrative officials cannot expand the law

to include activities that lack the essential element of consideration

(payment), as that would amount to rewriting the statute.

2. Tañada v. Tuvera, G.R. No. L-63915, 29 December 1986

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

-------

2) Who is the plaintiff: Lorenzo M. Tañada, Renato Constantino, Jose W.

Diokno, et al. (Petitioners)

3) Where the case file: G.R. No. L-63915, December 29, 1986 (Supreme

Court)

4) Who is the complainant/ peace officer/public officer: Lorenzo M.

Tañada and other public-spirited citizens and lawyers

5) Where MTC/MCTC the case prosecuted or what court where the case is

filed: Supreme Court of the Philippines (directly filed as a petition for

mandamus)

6) Who is the prosecutor execute the case trial: Petitioners prosecuted

the mandamus case directly; Respondents represented by the Solicitor

General

a. Whole Facts:

During the martial law era under President Ferdinand Marcos, numerous

presidential decrees, executive orders, letters of instructions, and

administrative rules were issued. Many of these decrees were never

published in the Official Gazette, yet the government enforced them as

active laws. Lorenzo Tañada and other civic-minded citizens filed a

petition for mandamus to compel the Executive Secretary, Juan C. Tuvera,

to publish these decrees in the Official Gazette. The respondents argued

that publication was not mandatory when the law itself provided for its

own date of effectivity (e.g., "This decree shall take effect immediately

upon approval").

a.1. Explanation of the Facts:

The government was enacting and enforcing secret laws under the guise of

presidential decrees without showing them to the public. Citizens sued to

force the government to print these laws, arguing that people cannot obey

laws they do not know exist. The government claimed that if a decree said

"effective immediately," it bypassed the publication requirement.

b. The Issue of the Case:

Whether publication in the Official Gazette is a mandatory requirement

for the effectivity of laws, presidential decrees, and administrative

rules, even if such laws declare they shall take effect "immediately."

b.1. Explanation of the Issue:

The court had to interpret Article 2 of the Civil Code: "Laws shall take

effect after fifteen days following the completion of their publication

in the Official Gazette, unless it is otherwise provided." The issue was

whether the phrase "unless it is otherwise provided" allowed the

legislature to skip publication entirely, or if it only allowed them to

change the 15-day period.

c. Case Violation in Statutory Construction:

The executive branch violated the fundamental rule of constitutional and

statutory construction by reading the clause "unless it is otherwise

provided" as an excuse to completely bypass the publication requirement.

This interpretation violated the due process clause of the Constitution

and the spirit of the Civil Code.

d. Court Ruling / Doctrine:

The Supreme Court ruled that publication is MANDATORY. The clause "unless

it is otherwise provided" refers to the *date* of effectivity (e.g.,

making it longer or shorter than 15 days, or making it immediate), but it

does NOT excuse the requirement of publication itself. All statutes,

presidential decrees, administrative rules of general applicability, and

charter laws must be published in full in the Official Gazette (or, per

subsequent laws, in a newspaper of general circulation) as a condition

for their effectivity. Unpublished laws have no legal force.

d.1. Explanation of Doctrinal Execution:

This doctrine is a cornerstone of the rule of law and due process. The

Supreme Court executed its power of interpretation to protect citizens

from secret legislation. By separating the "fact of publication" (which

is absolute) from the "timing of effectivity" (which can be varied), the

court harmonized the statute with the constitutional requirement of due

process, establishing that "ignorantia legis non excusat" (ignorance of

the law excuses no one) can only apply if the law is made publicly

accessible.

Applicable Study Guide Topic:

* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - A. Statutes

* II. PRELUDE TO THE EXERCISE OF THE POWER - A. Verba Legis Non Est

Recedendum

* II. PRELUDE TO THE EXERCISE OF THE POWER - B. Absoluta Sententia

Expositore Non Indigent

* Correlation Explanation: This case is directly correlated to "A.

Statutes" because it outlines the constitutional and statutory

prerequisites for a law's validity — specifically, the absolute requirement

of public notice. It is also applicable to the *Verba Legis* and

*Absoluta Sententia* doctrines because the court literally interpreted

Article 2 of the Civil Code, holding that the phrase "unless it is

otherwise provided" is clear in its limitation: it can only modify the

transition period of effectivity, but cannot be read to completely erase

the publication mandate.

3. Romualdez v. Sandiganbayan, G.R. No. 152259, July 29, 2004

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

-------

2) Who is the plaintiff: People of the Philippines (represented by the

Special Prosecutor)

3) Where the case file: G.R. No. 152259, July 29, 2004 (Supreme Court)

4) Who is the complainant/ peace officer/public officer: Presidential

Commission on Good Government (PCGG) / Ombudsman Investigators

5) Where MTC/MCTC the case prosecuted or what court where the case is

filed: Sandiganbayan (First Division)

6) Who is the prosecutor execute the case trial: Office of the Special

Prosecutor (OSP) under the Office of the Ombudsman

a. Whole Facts:

Alfredo Romualdez, a relative of former First Lady Imelda Marcos, was

charged with violating Section 5 of Republic Act No. 3019 (The Anti-Graft

and Corrupt Practices Act) for allegedly intervening in a government

contract with the National Power Corporation. Romualdez moved to quash

the information, arguing that Section 5 was unconstitutionally vague

because it did not define the terms "intervene" or "family relation." The

Sandiganbayan denied the motion. Romualdez petitioned the Supreme Court,

asserting a facial challenge against the graft law on the grounds of

vagueness.

a.1. Explanation of the Facts:

A relative of Imelda Marcos was prosecuted for corruption because he

allegedly intervened in a government deal. He tried to get the case

thrown out by arguing that the anti-graft law was so poorly written and

vague that no one could tell what "intervene" or "family relation" meant,

making the law unconstitutional.

b. The Issue of the Case:

Whether Section 5 of R.A. No. 3019 is unconstitutionally vague, and

whether a facial challenge based on the vagueness doctrine can be used to

strike down a penal statute.

b.1. Explanation of the Issue:

The court had to determine if the graft law violated due process by

failing to give fair warning of what conduct was prohibited. It also had

to decide if a criminal defendant could challenge a penal law "on its

face" (meaning the law is invalid in all its applications) rather than

"as applied" to his specific conduct.

c. Case Violation in Statutory Construction:

Romualdez attempted to misapply the "vagueness doctrine" by using a

facial challenge — a statutory construction tool reserved primarily for

First Amendment/free speech cases — to strike down a penal statute.

d. Court Ruling / Doctrine:

The Supreme Court ruled that Section 5 is constitutional.

* A facial challenge on the ground of vagueness is not applicable to

penal statutes; it can only be raised in "as-applied" cases, except in

free speech cases.

* The words "intervene" and "family relation" are not vague when read in

their common and statutory context. "Intervene" means to consciously

introduce oneself into a transaction, and "family relation" is defined

within the civil law context.

The motion to quash was denied.

d.1. Explanation of Doctrinal Execution:

The Supreme Court executed a strict limit on constitutional construction.

It established that courts must presume statutes are constitutional and

should not strike down penal laws on facial vagueness if they can be

reasonably interpreted in context. This prevents defendants from using

semantic arguments to escape prosecution, preserving the legislature's

intent to penalize graft.

Applicable Study Guide Topic:

* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - F. Ambiguity /

Vagueness

* VI. CONSTRUCTION OF CONSTITUTION AND SPECIFIC KINDS OF STATUTES - B.

Statutes Strictly Construed

* Correlation Explanation: This case is applicable to "F. Ambiguity /

Vagueness" because it defines the limits of the vagueness doctrine,

establishing that a law is not unconstitutionally vague simply because it

contains terms that require judicial interpretation. It applies to

"Statutes Strictly Construed" because it shows that penal laws and their

written scales must be applied literally as written, even if economic

inflation has made the punishment disproportionate over time.

4. Pesca v. Pesca, G.R. No. 136921, 17 April 2001

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

-------

2) Who is the plaintiff: Lorna G. Pesca (Petitioner/Plaintiff)

3) Where the case file: G.R. No. 136921, April 17, 2001 (Supreme Court)

4) Who is the complainant/ peace officer/public officer: Lorna G. Pesca

(wife who initiated the civil annulment case)

5) Where MTC/MCTC the case prosecuted or what court where the case is

filed: Regional Trial Court (RTC) of Pasig City (Branch 160)

6) Who is the prosecutor execute the case trial: Private counsel of Lorna

Pesca (civil trial); State represented by Public Prosecutor to prevent

collusion

a. Whole Facts:

Lorna Pesca filed a petition for the declaration of nullity of her

marriage to her husband, Lorimer Pesca, on the ground of psychological

incapacity under Article 36 of the Family Code. She presented evidence of

physical abuse and domestic violence. The trial court declared the

marriage void. The Court of Appeals reversed, citing the strict

guidelines established in the Supreme Court cases of *Santos v. Court of

Appeals* and *Republic v. Molina*. Lorna petitioned the Supreme Court,

arguing that the *Santos* and *Molina* guidelines should not apply

retroactively to her case, as her petition was filed before those

decisions were promulgated.

a.1. Explanation of the Facts:

Lorna sued to annul her marriage because of her husband's physical abuse,

claiming it proved "psychological incapacity." While her case was

ongoing, the Supreme Court laid down very strict guidelines in other

cases (*Santos* and *Molina*) defining psychological incapacity. Lorna

argued those guidelines should not be applied to her case because she

filed her lawsuit before those guidelines were written.

b. The Issue of the Case:

Whether the guidelines on psychological incapacity laid down in the

*Santos* and *Molina* cases should be applied retroactively to pending

cases.

b.1. Explanation of the Issue:

The court had to interpret the nature of judicial decisions under

statutory construction. Specifically, whether judicial interpretations of

a statute take effect only from the date of the decision (prospective) or

if they apply to all cases covered by the statute from its enactment

(retroactive).

c. Case Violation in Statutory Construction:

Lorna violated the rule on the nature of judicial interpretations by

arguing that Supreme Court decisions are equivalent to new statutes,

which are generally prospective.

d. Court Ruling / Doctrine:

The Supreme Court ruled that judicial decisions interpreting a law form

part of that law from the date of its enactment. The guidelines in

*Santos* and *Molina* do not constitute new laws but are merely

interpretations of what Article 36 of the Family Code has always meant

since its approval in 1988. Therefore, they apply retroactively to

Lorna's case. Physical abuse, while a ground for legal separation, does

not automatically constitute psychological incapacity under the

interpreted guidelines. The petition was denied.

d.1. Explanation of Doctrinal Execution:

The Supreme Court strongly executed the doctrine that judicial

interpretations are retroactive. Unlike legislative acts which create new

rules and are prospective, the judiciary's role is to declare what the

law has always meant. This reinforces the court's role as the supreme

expositor of statutory meaning, ensuring consistent application of the

law across all pending and future disputes.

Applicable Study Guide Topic:

* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - H. Legis

Interpretado Legis Vim Obtinet

* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - I. Kinds of

Construction

* Correlation Explanation: This case is the leading authority on the

maxim *Legis interpretado legis vim obtinet* (interpretation of the law

has the force of law) because it holds that Supreme Court decisions are

incorporated into the law itself. It directly correlates to the

prospectivity/retroactivity of construction, clarifying that judicial

interpretations must apply retroactively to pending disputes because the

court is not creating a new law but merely revealing the statute's

original, true meaning.

5. People v. Mapa, G.R. No. L-22301, 30 August 1967

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

-------

2) Who is the plaintiff: People of the Philippines (Plaintiff-Appellee)

3) Where the case file: G.R. No. L-22301, August 30, 1967 (Supreme Court)

4) Who is the complainant/ peace officer/public officer: Police officers

of the Batangas Police Department / Governor's office

5) Where MTC/MCTC the case prosecuted or what court where the case is

filed: Court of First Instance (CFI) of Batangas

6) Who is the prosecutor execute the case trial: Provincial Fiscal

(Prosecutor) of Batangas

a. Whole Facts:

Jose Mapa was charged with illegal possession of unlicensed firearm (a

home-made revolver). Mapa admitted possessing the firearm but presented

as his defense that he was appointed as a secret agent by the Governor of

Batangas. He argued that as a secret agent, he was an extension of the

peace officers of the province and was therefore exempt from the license

requirement under Section 879 of the Revised Administrative Code, which

exempts "peace officers."

a.1. Explanation of the Facts:

Jose Mapa was caught with an unlicensed gun. He argued he didn't need a

license because he was hired as a secret agent for the governor, which he

claimed made him a "peace officer" exempt from gun laws. The prosecution

argued that "secret agents" were not listed in the law's exemptions.

b. The Issue of the Case:

Whether a "secret agent" appointed by a provincial governor is included

in the definition of "peace officer" exempt from the firearm licensing

requirements under Section 879 of the Revised Administrative Code.

b.1. Explanation of the Issue:

The issue was whether the court could expand the plain language of the

gun exemption statute to include "secret agents," which were not

explicitly mentioned in the text but arguably performed similar duties to

peace officers.

c. Case Violation in Statutory Construction:

The defendant wanted the court to commit a violation of the plain meaning

rule by reading an unwritten exemption ("secret agent") into a highly

specific penal statute, which would amount to judicial legislation.

d. Court Ruling / Doctrine:

The Supreme Court ruled that Jose Mapa was GUILTY. The law is clear and

explicit: it exempts peace officers, and it does not mention secret

agents. When the law is clear, there is no room for interpretation — only

application. Courts cannot write into the law an exemption that the

legislature omitted. Mapa's conviction was affirmed.

d.1. Explanation of Doctrinal Execution:

This case is a classic example of the *Verba Legis* doctrine. The Supreme

Court executed its power by refusing to act as a legislature. It

established that in criminal statutes, exemptions must be construed

strictly. The court's ruling sends a strong message that it will strictly

enforce the literal words of the law to prevent individuals from creating

unauthorized legal defenses through administrative appointments.

Applicable Study Guide Topic:

* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - J. Extent of the

Power to Construe

* II. PRELUDE TO THE EXERCISE OF THE POWER - A. Verba Legis Non Est

Recedendum

* Correlation Explanation: This case is the absolute textbook

illustration of *Verba Legis Non Est Recedendum* (from the words of the

law there must be no departure). It is applicable because it strictly

limits the "Extent of the Power to Construe," establishing that courts

have zero authority to interpret, expand, or add words to a statute when

the text is already clear and unambiguous, even if a strict application

leads to a conviction.

6. Endencia v. David, G.R. No. L-6355-56, 31 August 1953

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

-------

2) Who is the plaintiff: Pastor M. Endencia and Sebastian T. Lopez

(Plaintiffs-Appellees)

3) Where the case file: G.R. No. L-6355-56, August 31, 1953 (Supreme

Court)

4) Who is the complainant/ peace officer/public officer: Saturnino David

(Collector of Internal Revenue who initiated tax assessments)

5) Where MTC/MCTC the case prosecuted or what court where the case is

filed: Court of First Instance (CFI) of Manila

6) Who is the prosecutor execute the case trial: Solicitor General

representing the Collector of Internal Revenue

a. Whole Facts:

In the case of *Perfecto v. Meer*, the Supreme Court ruled that taxing

the salaries of judicial officers (judges and justices) was

unconstitutional because it amounted to a decrease in their compensation,

violating Article VIII, Section 9 of the 1935 Constitution. In response,

Congress enacted Republic Act No. 590, which contained Section 13,

explicitly declaring that the taxing of judicial salaries shall not be

construed as a diminution of their compensation. The Collector of

Internal Revenue, Saturnino David, proceeded to assess taxes on the

salaries of Justices Pastor Endencia and others. The Justices challenged

the constitutionality of Section 13.

a.1. Explanation of the Facts:

The Supreme Court previously ruled that taxing judges' salaries was

unconstitutional. Congress got angry and passed a new law declaring that

taxing judges' salaries is NOT unconstitutional. The tax collector then

tried to tax the judges. The judges sued, arguing Congress cannot

overrule a Supreme Court interpretation of the Constitution by passing a

declaratory law.

b. The Issue of the Case:

Whether Section 13 of R.A. No. 590, which interprets the constitutional

provision on judicial compensation, is constitutional.

b.1. Explanation of the Issue:

The core issue was which branch has the ultimate authority to interpret

the Constitution and statutes. Can Congress pass a law defining how a

constitutional provision must be construed, thereby overriding a prior

Supreme Court interpretation?

c. Case Violation in Statutory Construction:

Congress violated the principle of separation of powers by attempting to

perform a judicial function. By passing a statute that declared how the

Constitution should be interpreted, Congress engaged in "legislative

interpretation," which encroached upon the exclusive situs of statutory

and constitutional construction.

d. Court Ruling / Doctrine:

The Supreme Court ruled that Section 13 of R.A. No. 590 is

UNCONSTITUTIONAL. The interpretation of the Constitution and statutes is

an exclusive judicial function. Congress has the power to enact laws, but

once a law is enacted, the power to interpret it and determine its

constitutionality belongs solely to the courts. Congress cannot overrule

judicial construction through legislative declaration.

d.1. Explanation of Doctrinal Execution:

This is a defining case for the independence of the judiciary. The

Supreme Court strongly executed the doctrine of judicial supremacy in

interpretation. It established that the legislature cannot dictate to the

courts how to construe laws or the Constitution. This prevents the

legislature from bypassing constitutional checks and balances through

declaratory statutes.

Applicable Study Guide Topic:

* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - C. Situs of

Construction

* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - G. Statutory

Construction v. Judicial Legislation

* Correlation Explanation: This case directly defines the "Situs of

Construction," establishing that the power to interpret the Constitution

and laws belongs exclusively to the judicial department. It violates

"Statutory Construction v. Judicial Legislation" because it invalidates

Congress's attempt to engage in legislative interpretation, ruling that

the legislature cannot dictate statutory meaning to the courts or

overrule judicial rulings through declaratory statutes.

7. People v. Corpuz, G.R. No. 180016, 29 April 2014

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

-------

2) Who is the plaintiff: People of the Philippines (Plaintiff-Appellee)

3) Where the case file: G.R. No. 180016, April 29, 2014 (Supreme Court)

4) Who is the complainant/ peace officer/public officer: Lialinda Macoto

(private complainant/victim of Estafa)

5) Where MTC/MCTC the case prosecuted or what court where the case is

filed: Regional Trial Court (RTC) of Manila (Branch 46)

6) Who is the prosecutor execute the case trial: Public Prosecutor of

Manila assisted by private prosecutor

a. Whole Facts:

Danilo Corpuz was charged with Estafa under Article 315 of the Revised

Penal Code. He received pieces of jewelry from Lialinda Macoto to sell on

commission, but failed to return the jewelry or remit the proceeds. The

value of the jewelry was P98,000. Corpuz was convicted, and the penalty

imposed was a high prison term based on the penalty scale written in the

Revised Penal Code of 1932, which calculated penalties based on the value

of the property stolen. Corpuz appealed, arguing that the penalty was

unconstitutional as it was cruel and unusual, given that P98,000 in 1932

had a vastly different purchasing power than in 2014.

a.1. Explanation of the Facts:

Corpuz stole jewelry worth P98,000. Under the old 1932 penal law, because

P98,000 was a massive sum back then, he was sentenced to a very long

prison term (up to 15 years). He argued that the court should adjust the

penalty scale to account for inflation, as P98,000 today is worth much

less, making his long sentence unfair.

b. The Issue of the Case:

Whether the courts can adjust the penalties written in a penal statute

(Article 315 of the RPC) to account for inflation and the changed value

of money over time.

b.1. Explanation of the Issue:

The court had to decide if it could apply "progressive interpretation" or

equity to modify the penalty scales of a penal law, or if it was bound to

apply the literal terms written in 1932 until Congress amended the law.

c. Case Violation in Statutory Construction:

The appellant urged the court to engage in judicial legislation by

rewriting the penalty thresholds of the Revised Penal Code, which would

violate the rule of separation of powers and the *Verba Legis* doctrine.

d. Court Ruling / Doctrine:

The Supreme Court ruled that it CANNOT adjust the penalties. The law is

clear as to the monetary thresholds, and courts cannot modify the law

based on inflation. The power to define crimes and prescribe penalties

belongs exclusively to the legislature. The court affirmed the conviction

but strongly urged Congress to amend the outdated penalty scales (which

Congress eventually did in 2017 via R.A. 10951).

d.1. Explanation of Doctrinal Execution:

The Supreme Court executed a strict self-restraint policy. It established

that even if a penal law has become obsolete or harsh due to economic

inflation, the court cannot step into the shoes of the legislature to

rewrite it. This preserves the constitutional separation of powers,

forcing the legislature to perform its duty while the court remains a

strict applicator of the written text.

Applicable Study Guide Topic:

* I. BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY OF STATUTORY CONSTRUCTION - G. Statutory

Construction v. Judicial Legislation

* VI. CONSTRUCTION OF CONSTITUTION AND SPECIFIC KINDS OF STATUTES - B.

Statutes Strictly Construed

* Correlation Explanation: This case is applicable to "G. Statutory

Construction v. Judicial Legislation" because it enforces strict

boundaries on judicial power, declaring that adjusting penal sentences

for inflation is a legislative policy decision, and judicial adjustment

would constitute illegal legislative encroachment. It applies to

"Statutes Strictly Construed" because it shows that penal laws and their

written scales must be applied literally as written, even if economic

Two siblings sold their inherited land shares. The buyer moved in and

perpetrate an injustice. The court ruled that "we must not allow the law

to be used as a shield for bad faith." It established that actual notice

can substitute for written notice when the passage of time makes a

literal interpretation completely absurd.
