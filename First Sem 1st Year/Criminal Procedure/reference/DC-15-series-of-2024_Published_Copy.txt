Manila, Philippines

DEPARTMENT CIRCULAR NO. 015
Series of 2024

DATE: July 16, 2024

SUBJECT: 2024 DOJ-NPS RULES ON PRELIMINARY INVESTIGATIONS AND INQUEST PROCEEDINGS

================================================================================
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================
Department Circular No. 015 (DC 15) establishes the "2024 DOJ-NPS Rules on Preliminary 
Investigations and Inquest Proceedings." These Rules modernize, standardize, and govern 
the National Prosecution Service (NPS) in preliminary investigations and inquests for 
crimes where the law prescribes a penalty of at least six (6) years and one (1) day.

================================================================================
RULE I: TITLE, SCOPE, COVERAGE, AND POLICIES
================================================================================
* Section 1. Title: "2024 DOJ-NPS Rules on Preliminary Investigations and Inquest Proceedings"
* Section 2. Scope: Governs the conduct of preliminary investigations and inquest proceedings 
  in all prosecution offices of the National Prosecution Service (NPS) under R.A. No. 10071, 
  subject to D.C. No. 20 (Policy on Pro-Active Involvement of Prosecutors in Case Build-Up).
* Section 3. Coverage: Preliminary investigations are required for crimes where the prescribed 
  penalty is at least six (6) years and one (1) day, regardless of the fine.
* Motu Proprio Dismissal: Investigating prosecutors can dismiss complaints at any stage if 
  they find no "prima facie evidence with reasonable certainty of conviction," subject to 
  prior approval by the head of the prosecution office.

================================================================================
RULE II: QUANTUM OF EVIDENCE
================================================================================
* Section 5. Quantum of Evidence: raised from "probable cause" to "prima facie evidence 
  with reasonable certainty of conviction."
* Definition: Prima facie evidence with reasonable certainty of conviction exists when 
  the evidence-at-hand (testimonial, documentary, or real) is, if left uncontradicted, 
  sufficient to establish all elements of the crime beyond reasonable doubt, and the 
  identity of the accused.
* Evaluation: Prosecutors must verify that evidence is admissible, credible, and capable 
  of being preserved and presented at trial.

================================================================================
PROCEDURAL TIMELINE & WORKFLOW
================================================================================
1. Investigation Phase:
   * The investigating prosecutor must resolve the preliminary investigation within sixty 
     (60) calendar days from assignment.
   * A maximum extension of thirty (30) calendar days is allowed only in cases involving 
     capital offenses, complex legal issues, counter-charges, consolidation, or case 
     reassignment.
2. Transmittal Phase:
   * The recommendatory resolution and the corresponding information must be transmitted 
     to the Head of the Prosecution Office within five (5) calendar days from the date 
     of the resolution.
3. Review and Approval:
   * The Head of the Prosecution Office must review and act upon (approve/disapprove) 
     the resolution within ten (10) calendar days from receipt.
4. Promulgation:
   * Resolutions must be promulgated within five (5) calendar days from approval.

================================================================================
MODERNIZATION & DIGITAL REFORMS
================================================================================
* E-Filing: Complaining and responding parties may file submissions electronically.
* Virtual Proceedings: Preliminary investigations, witness oaths, and inquests may be 
  conducted online via videoconferencing as alternatives to in-person sessions.

================================================================================
CONSTITUTIONAL VALIDITY
================================================================================
* The Supreme Court of the Philippines upheld the validity of DC 15 in G.R. No. 280455 
  (Atty. Meking v. Remulla), declaring that preliminary investigations are executive functions, 
  granting the DOJ authority to establish its own evidentiary and prosecutorial rules.
