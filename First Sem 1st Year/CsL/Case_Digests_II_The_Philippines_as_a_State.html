1. THE HOLY SEE v. HON. ERIBERTO U. ROSARIO, JR., ET AL.

Citation: G.R. No. 102349, 238 SCRA 524

Promulgation Date: December 1, 1994

Ponente: Quiason, J.

Syllabus Topic: Doctrine of State Immunity (Art. XVI, Sec. 3) — Entitlement of immunity, justiciable or

political question?

Facts

The Holy See is an international juridical person represented in the Philippines by the Papal Nuncio. It

owned a contiguous parcel of land in Parañaque (Lot 5-A), while the Philippine Realty Corporation (PRC)

owned the adjoining Lots 5-B and 5-D. These lots were leased to Ramon Licup, who assigned his lease

rights to Tropicana Properties and Development Corporation. In 1989, the Holy See agreed to sell Lot 5-A

to Star Group International, Inc. for ₱1,240 per square meter, conditioned upon the clearing of squatters

from the lot. The squatters refused to vacate, and Tropicana claimed a right of first refusal.

Consequently, Star Group filed an action for specific performance, reconveyance, and damages in the

Regional Trial Court (RTC) of Makati against the Holy See, PRC, and Tropicana. The Holy See filed a

Motion to Dismiss based on sovereign immunity. The RTC denied the motion, ruling that by entering into a

commercial sale of land, the Holy See had stepped down to the level of a private individual. The Holy See

filed a petition for certiorari with the Supreme Court. The Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA) intervened,

certifying that the Holy See is an immune foreign sovereign and that the acquisition and disposal of the

property was intended for the official residence of the Papal Nuncio, an act jure imperii.

Issue

1. Whether the Holy See is immune from suit in Philippine courts under the Doctrine of State

Immunity.

2. Whether the Executive Department's determination through the DFA certifying

diplomatic/sovereign immunity is a political question binding upon the courts.

Supreme Court Ruling

YES to both. The Supreme Court reversed the trial court and dismissed Civil Case No. 90-183 against the

Holy See.

Explanation of the Court's Ruling

1. Status of the Holy See: Under international law, the Holy See possesses an exceptional universal

character and full international legal personality recognized by the community of nations. It has treaty-

making capacity, right of legation, and full diplomatic status.

2. Restrictive Doctrine of State Immunity (*Jure Imperii* vs. *Jure Gestionis*): The mere

fact that the Holy See engaged in a contract to sell land does not automatically make it a commercial

enterprise (jure gestionis). The property was acquired for the establishment of the Apostolic

Nunciature (the official residence and chancery of the Papal Nuncio). When the site proved unsuitable

due to the squatters, the decision to dispose of the property was directly connected to the sovereign

function of maintaining its diplomatic mission.

3. Political Question Doctrine: The determination of whether an entity is entitled to sovereign

immunity is an executive prerogative. The DFA's formal certification that the Holy See enjoys sovereign

immunity and that the transaction was jure imperii is an act of state in foreign relations that is

conclusive and binding upon domestic courts.

Correlation and Application under "The Philippines as a State"

Under the element of Sovereignty (*External Sovereignty*) and the constitutional adoption of

generally accepted principles of international law (Art. II, Sec. 2), the Philippine State respects the

sovereign equality of all foreign States (Par in parem non habet imperium). Domestic courts cannot usurp

the executive branch's authority in conducting diplomatic foreign affairs.

Why the Doctrine was Used & Specific Line Applied

• Doctrine Used: Sovereign Immunity of Foreign States / Political Question Doctrine.

• Specific Line of Doctrine: Par in parem non habet imperium (An equal has no authority over an

equal); determination of diplomatic status and sovereign immunity by the DFA is a political

question binding on the judiciary.

2. LIANG (HUEFENG) v. PEOPLE OF THE PHILIPPINES

Citation: G.R. No. 125865, 323 SCRA 652 (Jan. 28, 2000); Resolution on Reconsideration, 355 SCRA 125

(Mar. 26, 2001)

Promulgation Date: January 28, 2000 / March 26, 2001

Ponente: Ynares-Santiago, J.

Syllabus Topic: Doctrine of State Immunity — Entitlement of immunity, justiciable or political question?

/ Functional Immunity

Facts

Jeffrey Liang, a Chinese national, was employed as an Economist by the Asian Development Bank (ADB),

an international organization headquartered in Mandaluyong City. Two criminal informations for Grave

Oral Defamation (slander) were filed against Liang in the Metropolitan Trial Court (MeTC) of

Mandaluyong for allegedly uttering malicious and defamatory words against a fellow ADB employee, Joyce

Cabal.

The MeTC dismissed the informations based on a DFA endorsement stating that Liang was entitled to

diplomatic immunity under Section 45 of the ADB Headquarters Agreement. The RTC set aside the

dismissal on certiorari. Liang filed a petition for review with the Supreme Court, insisting that the DFA's

endorsement established absolute immunity from criminal prosecution for words spoken during his

tenure.

Issue

Whether an officer or international official of the ADB is entitled to absolute diplomatic immunity from

criminal prosecution for oral defamation.

Supreme Court Ruling

NO. The Supreme Court denied Liang's petition and ordered the trial court to proceed with the criminal

prosecution.

Explanation of the Court's Ruling

1. Functional Immunity, Not Absolute Immunity: Under Section 45 of the ADB Headquarters

Agreement, officers and staff of the ADB enjoy immunity from legal process only with respect to acts

performed by them in their official capacity.

2. Defamation Is Not an Official Function: Slandering or defaming a person can never be part of

the official functions of an international economist. The commission of a crime or tortious act is an

ultra vires personal act outside official duties.

3. Due Process & Trial Determination: The invocation of immunity under an international

headquarters agreement is not self-executing for private criminal acts. The question of whether an act

was committed in an official capacity is a justiciable question of fact to be determined by the trial court

during trial, not by a preliminary DFA certification.

Correlation and Application under "The Philippines as a State"

While the Philippine State recognizes the privileges and immunities of international organizations to

enable them to perform their mandate, this does not grant foreign officials a license to violate Philippine

criminal laws (Territorial Principle of Criminal Law). State sovereignty requires that municipal courts

have the authority to try offenses committed within Philippine territory when the acts are outside official

international functions.

Why the Doctrine was Used & Specific Line Applied

• Doctrine Used: Functional Immunity of International Organization Officials (Ratione Materiae).

• Specific Line of Doctrine: Immunity of international officials covers only acts performed in their

official capacity; immunity cannot be invoked to shield criminal or tortious acts (*ultra

vires*) against private individuals.

3. MOST REV. PEDRO D. ARIGO, ET AL. v. SCOTT H. SWIFT, ET AL.

Citation: G.R. No. 206501, 735 SCRA 102

Promulgation Date: September 16, 2014

Ponente: Villarama, Jr., J.

Syllabus Topic: When a suit is against a state and when it is not / Suit against public officers

Facts

On January 17, 2013, the USS Guardian, an Avenger-class mine countermeasures ship of the US Navy, ran

aground on the South Atoll of the Tubbataha Reefs in the Sulu Sea, a UNESCO World Heritage site and a

protected Marine Protected Area under RA 10067. The grounding caused extensive physical destruction to

2,345.67 square meters of pristine coral reefs.

Petitioners (Bishop Pedro Arigo, environmental groups, and public interest lawyers) filed a direct petition

for the issuance of a Writ of Kalikasan with prayer for a Temporary Environmental Protection Order

(TEPO) against US military officers (Admiral Scott Swift, Commander Mark Rice, etc.) and Philippine

Executive officials. Petitioners demanded environmental rehabilitation, payment of fines under RA 10067,

review of the Visiting Forces Agreement (VFA), and criminal/civil proceedings against the US

commanders.

Issue

1. Whether the US military officers (Swift, Rice, et al.) are immune from suit under the Doctrine of

State Immunity.

2. Whether a suit against foreign military officers commanding a warship is in reality a suit against the

United States Government.

Supreme Court Ruling

YES. The Supreme Court dismissed the petition as against the US respondents on the ground of State

Immunity.

Explanation of the Court's Ruling

1. Real Party-in-Interest: Although individual US military officers were named as respondents, the

USS Guardian is a commissioned naval warship of the United States. The acts of the commanding

officers were performed in the official navigation and operation of a military vessel in connection with

sovereign defense activities (acta jure imperii).

2. Suit Against the State: A suit against public or military officers for acts done in the discharge of

governmental/military functions is a suit against the foreign State itself, because the satisfaction of any

judgment or order would ultimately fall upon the foreign State and require affirmative acts from its

treasury and government.

3. No Waiver in the VFA: The Visiting Forces Agreement contains no waiver of US sovereign

immunity from civil suits before Philippine courts. Under customary international law, foreign

warships enjoy sovereign immunity, and redress for environmental damage caused by foreign state

vessels must be pursued through diplomatic channels and state-to-state negotiation under

international law, not domestic litigation.

Correlation and Application under "The Philippines as a State"

Demonstrates the interplay between the Philippine State's territorial sovereignty over its marine territory

(Article I) and the international law principle of sovereign equality of States (Par in parem non habet

imperium). While the Philippines has absolute sovereignty over Tubbataha Reefs, municipal courts cannot

acquire in personam jurisdiction over foreign military commanders acting in a sovereign capacity.

Why the Doctrine was Used & Specific Line Applied

• Doctrine Used: Sovereign Immunity of Foreign States / Non-Suability of Military Command.

• Specific Line of Doctrine: A suit against public/military officers for acts performed in

their official military capacity is a suit against the State itself and is barred without express

sovereign consent.

4. SOUTHEAST ASIAN FISHERIES DEVELOPMENT CENTER (SEAFDEC)

v. NLRC AND DANILO ACOSTA

Citation: G.R. No. 86773, 241 SCRA 580

Promulgation Date: February 14, 1995

Ponente: Vitug, J.

Syllabus Topic: Immunity of International Organizations and Agencies

Facts

Danilo Acosta was hired as a Research Associate by SEAFDEC Aquaculture Department (AQD) in

Tigbauan, Iloilo. SEAFDEC is an international intergovernmental organization established in 1967 by an

international treaty entered into by the Philippines, Japan, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Vietnam.

Acosta was dismissed from service due to alleged financial irregularities. He filed a complaint for illegal

dismissal and reinstatement with the National Labor Relations Commission (NLRC) Regional Arbitration

Branch in Iloilo City.

SEAFDEC moved to dismiss the complaint on the ground that, as an international agency, it is immune

from the jurisdiction of local labor tribunals. The Labor Arbiter and the NLRC denied the motion to

dismiss, asserting jurisdiction over employment contracts executed within Philippine territory.

Issue

Whether SEAFDEC enjoys sovereign immunity from the jurisdiction of the Philippine labor tribunals

(NLRC).

Supreme Court Ruling

YES. The Supreme Court granted SEAFDEC's petition, set aside the NLRC resolutions, and dismissed the

labor complaint.

Explanation of the Court's Ruling

1. International Personality: SEAFDEC was established by treaty among sovereign nations to

conduct regional research on fisheries and aquaculture. The Philippine Government is a signatory and

host state.

2. Purpose of Immunity: The grant of diplomatic privileges and immunities to international

organizations is essential to protect them from unilateral control or interference by the host state.

Submitting international organizations to local labor tribunals would allow local authorities to dictate

their internal administration and disrupt their international mission.

3. No Local Jurisdiction: The NLRC has no jurisdiction over employment disputes involving

international organizations enjoying immunity, unless there is an express waiver.

Correlation and Application under "The Philippines as a State"

Under the Incorporation Clause (Art. II, Sec. 2), the Philippine State adopts international law as part

of the law of the land. The State honors its treaty commitments (Pacta sunt servanda) to respect the

jurisdictional immunity of international intergovernmental agencies operating in the country.

Why the Doctrine was Used & Specific Line Applied

• Doctrine Used: Diplomatic Immunity of International Intergovernmental Organizations.

• Specific Line of Doctrine: International organizations are immune from local labor

jurisdiction to safeguard their independence and international public mandate.

5. ERNESTO CALLADO v. INTERNATIONAL RICE RESEARCH INSTITUTE

(IRRI) AND NLRC

Citation: G.R. No. 106483, 244 SCRA 210

Promulgation Date: May 22, 1995

Ponente: Romero, J.

Syllabus Topic: Immunity of International Organizations and Agencies

Facts

Ernesto Callado was employed as a driver by the International Rice Research Institute (IRRI) in Los Baños,

Laguna. While driving an IRRI vehicle on official business, Callado figured in an accident that caused

vehicular damage and physical injuries. IRRI terminated Callado's employment for negligence. Callado

filed a complaint for illegal dismissal before the Labor Arbiter.

IRRI invoked diplomatic immunity from legal process pursuant to Presidential Decree No. 1620,

which granted IRRI the status of an international organization with full immunity from every form of legal

process in the Philippines. The Labor Arbiter dismissed the complaint, and the NLRC affirmed the

dismissal. Callado petitioned the Supreme Court, arguing that PD 1620 is unconstitutional as it denies

citizens access to courts.

Issue

Whether IRRI enjoys immunity from the jurisdiction of Philippine labor tribunals pursuant to PD 1620.

Supreme Court Ruling

YES. The Supreme Court dismissed Callado's petition and upheld IRRI's immunity.

Explanation of the Court's Ruling

1. Statutory & Treaty Recognition: PD 1620 expressly recognized IRRI as an international

organization with juridical personality, granting it immunity from legal process except to the extent

that it expressly waives such immunity.

2. Constitutionality of Immunity: The grant of immunity to international organizations does not

violate the constitutional guarantee of access to courts (Art. III, Sec. 16). Immunity is a recognized limit

on municipal jurisdiction based on the sovereign prerogative of the State to enter into international

agreements and host international scientific bodies.

3. Absence of Waiver: IRRI did not waive its immunity. Entering into an employment contract in

the Philippines does not constitute an implied waiver of immunity.

Correlation and Application under "The Philippines as a State"

The Philippine State exercises its sovereign power through the legislature (or presidential decree with

legislative force) to confer diplomatic immunities to promote international scientific research and

international cooperation.

Why the Doctrine was Used & Specific Line Applied

• Doctrine Used: Immunity of International Research Organizations under Special Law (PD 1620).

• Specific Line of Doctrine: International agencies enjoy complete immunity from

domestic legal process unless there is an express waiver enacted by their authorized governing

body.

6. DEPARTMENT OF FOREIGN AFFAIRS (DFA) v. NLRC AND JOSE C.

FELIX

Citation: G.R. No. 113191, 261 SCRA 776

Promulgation Date: September 18, 1996

Ponente: Vitug, J.

Syllabus Topic: Immunity of International Organizations / Political Question Doctrine

Facts

Jose C. Felix was hired by the Asian Development Bank (ADB) as a maintenance technician under a service

contract. When ADB terminated his services, Felix filed an illegal dismissal complaint before the Labor

Arbiter against ADB. ADB did not submit to the tribunal's jurisdiction, but instead referred the summons

to the DFA.

The DFA formally wrote to the Labor Arbiter, advising that the ADB and its officers enjoy immunity from

legal process under Article 50 of the ADB Charter and Section 44 of the Headquarters Agreement. Despite

the DFA's official communication, the Labor Arbiter declared ADB in default and rendered judgment

ordering ADB to pay backwages and damages. The NLRC upheld the Labor Arbiter. The DFA filed a

petition for certiorari with the Supreme Court on behalf of ADB.

Issue

Whether the NLRC committed grave abuse of discretion in asserting jurisdiction over the ADB despite the

DFA's formal certification of diplomatic immunity.

Supreme Court Ruling

YES. The Supreme Court set aside the NLRC decision and ordered the dismissal of the labor case against

ADB.

Explanation of the Court's Ruling

1. Binding Character of DFA Certification: The DFA is the executive department tasked with the

conduct of foreign relations. When the DFA formally recognizes and certifies that an entity possesses

international legal personality and is entitled to diplomatic immunity, that determination is a political

question that is conclusive upon courts and quasi-judicial agencies.

2. Charter and Headquarters Agreement: The ADB Charter (ratified by the Philippine Senate)

and the ADB Headquarters Agreement are binding international treaties having the force of domestic

law.

3. Remedy of Aggrieved Employees: The immunity of international organizations is not an

absolute denial of justice; the ADB maintains an internal grievance mechanism and Administrative

Tribunal to resolve personnel disputes.

Correlation and Application under "The Philippines as a State"

Highlights the principle of Separation of Powers within the Philippine State. The Judiciary and

administrative agencies cannot review or overturn executive policy determinations regarding foreign state

and international organization immunities.

Why the Doctrine was Used & Specific Line Applied

• Doctrine Used: Conclusiveness of Executive Certification on Diplomatic Immunity.

• Specific Line of Doctrine: DFA's certification of diplomatic immunity is a political act

that ousts the jurisdiction of local labor courts.

7. SPOUSES FONTANILLA v. INGRACIO MALIAMAN AND NATIONAL

IRRIGATION ADMINISTRATION (NIA)

Citation: G.R. Nos. L-55963 & L-61045, 194 SCRA 486

Promulgation Date: February 27, 1991

Ponente: Paras, J.

Syllabus Topic: Immunity of Incorporated Government Agencies / Proprietary Functions

Facts

A pickup truck owned by the National Irrigation Administration (NIA) and driven by its official driver,

Ingracio Maliaman, struck and killed Francisco Fontanilla, the minor son of the petitioner spouses, along

the national highway in San Jose City, Nueva Ecija. The parents filed an action for damages against

Maliaman and the NIA.

NIA claimed state immunity from suit, asserting that it is a government agency performing governmental

functions and cannot be held liable for the torts of its drivers under Article 2180 of the Civil Code because

the driver was not a "special agent." The trial court held NIA liable, but on initial appeal, the CA reversed.

The Supreme Court took cognizance of the case.

Issue

Whether the National Irrigation Administration (an incorporated government agency) can claim immunity

from suit and liability for the tortious acts of its driver.

Supreme Court Ruling

NO. The Supreme Court held NIA liable for damages under Article 2180 of the Civil Code.

Explanation of the Court's Ruling

1. Incorporated Agency with Power to Sue and Be Sued: NIA is a government-owned and

controlled corporation (GOCC) created under Republic Act No. 3601 with a separate juridical

personality. Section 2(f) of its charter explicitly confers upon it the power "to sue and be sued in

any court." This is an express legislative waiver of state immunity.

2. Proprietary Function: While irrigation projects serve the public, NIA operates like a private

enterprise by collecting irrigation fees and service charges from farmers. It performs proprietary and

corporate functions (jure gestionis).

3. Application of Article 2180, NCC: When an incorporated government agency engages in

proprietary business, it assumes the responsibilities and liabilities of an ordinary employer under the

law of torts.

Correlation and Application under "The Philippines as a State"

The State can create autonomous corporate entities (GOCCs) to conduct infrastructure and economic

activities. In doing so, the State exercises its power to expressly waive immunity in the corporate charter to

ensure commercial accountability.

Why the Doctrine was Used & Specific Line Applied

• Doctrine Used: Express Waiver of Immunity by Corporate Charter ("To sue and be sued").

• Specific Line of Doctrine: Incorporated government agencies performing proprietary

functions under a charter with a "sue and be sued" clause are liable for torts committed by

their employees.

8. RAMON FAROLAN (COMMISSIONER OF CUSTOMS) v. COURT OF TAX

APPEALS AND BAGONG BUHAY TRADING

Citation: G.R. No. 42204, 217 SCRA 298

Promulgation Date: January 21, 1993

Ponente: Bidin, J.

Syllabus Topic: Immunity of Unincorporated Government Agencies / Governmental Functions

Facts

Bagong Buhay Trading imported 63 bales of polyester fabric. The Collector of Customs seized the goods for

alleged undervaluation and smuggling. Bagong Buhay appealed to the Court of Tax Appeals (CTA), which

declared the seizure illegal and ordered the Collector and the Commissioner of Customs (Ramon Farolan)

to release the goods upon payment of duties.

When Bagong Buhay attempted to claim the goods from the customs warehouse, only 17 bales were found;

the remaining 46 bales were missing, and the 17 bales were badly damaged by humidity, pests, and

exposure. Bagong Buhay moved to hold the Commissioner of Customs and the Bureau of Customs liable for

the value of the missing and damaged goods. The CTA ordered the Bureau of Customs and its officers to

pay the damages.

Issue

Whether the Bureau of Customs (an unincorporated government agency) and its officers are liable for

damages resulting from the loss and deterioration of goods in customs custody while performing sovereign

governmental functions.

Supreme Court Ruling

NO. The Supreme Court reversed the CTA and ruled that the Bureau of Customs is immune from suit and

liability.

Explanation of the Court's Ruling

1. Unincorporated Agency Performing Governmental Functions: The Bureau of Customs is

an unincorporated agency of the national government exercising purely sovereign and governmental

functions (jure imperii), specifically the assessment and collection of customs duties, trade

enforcement, and prevention of smuggling.

3. Personal Liability: The suit for personal tortious conduct is a suit against the officers in their

individual capacity, not a suit against the United States Government. Therefore, State Immunity is

inapplicable.

Correlation and Application under "The Philippines as a State"

The Philippine State protects its citizens from oppressive, unlawful acts committed within its territory. The

doctrine of State Immunity cannot be subverted into an instrument of tyranny or personal immunity for

wrongdoers.

Why the Doctrine was Used & Specific Line Applied

• Doctrine Used: Personal Liability of Public Officers for Ultra Vires / Tortious Acts.

• Specific Line of Doctrine: "State immunity cannot be used to perpetrate an injustice on

a citizen." Officers committing torts or crimes act beyond their authority and are personally liable.
