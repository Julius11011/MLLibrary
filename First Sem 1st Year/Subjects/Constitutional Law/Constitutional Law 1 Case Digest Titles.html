CONSTITUTIONAL LAW 1

Masterlist of Assigned Landmark Cases & Digest Titles - Paragraph Edition

★ MASTER CASE DIRECTORY - PARAGRAPH COMPENDIUM Format: Paragraph Reading Edition (Non-Tabular Continuous Layout) Course: Constitutional Law 1 (1987 Philippine Constitution) Total Landmark Cases: 149 Assigned Supreme Court Decisions (Topics I to VII) Each entry contains: Case Number, Complete Title, Official Citation, Promulgation Date, and Key Constitutional Doctrine / Principle.

Topic I: The Philippine Constitution

Subject Scope: Amendments, Revision, Ratification, Effectivity, and Judicial Review • 10 Assigned Cases

CASE 1

De Leon vs. Esguerra

CITATION Citation: 153 SCRA 602, No. L-78059, August 31, 1987

Key Legal Doctrine: Effectivity of the 1987 Constitution upon ratification on February 2, 1987 (Art. XVIII, Sec. 27); designation of barangay officials.

CASE 2

Lambino vs. COMELEC

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 174153, October 25, 2006

Key Legal Doctrine: Difference between Amendment and Revision; Two-Part Test (Quantitative and Qualitative Test); People's Initiative requirements.

CASE 3

Imbong vs. COMELEC

CITATION Citation: 35 SCRA 28 (1970)

Key Legal Doctrine: Authority of Congress acting as a constituent assembly to call a Constitutional Convention (Art. XVII, Sec. 3).

CASE 4

Defensor-Santiago vs. COMELEC

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 127325, March 19, 1997

Key Legal Doctrine: Inadequacy of RA 6735 for Constitutional Amendments via People's Initiative; 12%/3% registered voter requirements and 5-year bar.

CASE 5

Lambino vs. COMELEC (Resolution on MR)

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 174153, November 21, 2006

Key Legal Doctrine: Affirmation of dismissal of People's Initiative petition; requirement of direct proposal by the people.

CASE 6

Gonzales vs. COMELEC

CITATION Citation: 21 SCRA 774 (1967)

Key Legal Doctrine: Submission of proposed constitutional amendments simultaneously with regular general elections is permissible.

CASE 7

Occeña vs. COMELEC

CITATION Citation: 104 SCRA 1 (1981)

Key Legal Doctrine: Adequacy of time for voters to study proposed constitutional amendments prior to plebiscite.

CASE 8

Almario vs. Alba

CITATION Citation: 127 SCRA 69 (1984)

Key Legal Doctrine: Doctrine of proper submission in constitutional plebiscites; voters' freedom of choice.

CASE 9

Tolentino vs. COMELEC

CITATION Citation: 41 SCRA 702 (1971)

Key Legal Doctrine: Doctrine of Proper Submission; prohibition against piecemeal ratification of constitutional revisions.

CASE 10

Javellana vs. Executive Secretary

CITATION Citation: 50 SCRA 30 (1973)

Key Legal Doctrine: Judicial review of constitutional amendments, political question doctrine, and validity of ratification via Citizens Assemblies.

Topic II: The Philippines as a State

Subject Scope: National Territory, Sovereignty, State Immunity, and Exceptions • 19 Assigned Cases

CASE 11

The Holy See vs. Rosario

CITATION Citation: 238 SCRA 524, December 1, 1994

Key Legal Doctrine: State Immunity of foreign sovereign states; distinction between acts jure imperii (sovereign) and jure gestionis (commercial).

CASE 12

Liang vs. People

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 125865, January 28, 2000

Key Legal Doctrine: Immunity of international agency officials (Asian Development Bank); defamatory acts not covered by official acts immunity.

CASE 13

Arigo vs. Swift

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 206501, September 16, 2014

Key Legal Doctrine: USS Guardian grounding on Tubbataha Reef; state immunity of foreign military commanders performing sovereign military functions; VFA application.

CASE 14

SEAFDEC vs. NLRC

CITATION Citation: 241 SCRA 580 (1995)

Key Legal Doctrine: Immunity of international organizations from labor jurisdiction and domestic tribunal processes.

CASE 15

Callado vs. IRRI

CITATION Citation: 244 SCRA 210 (1995)

Key Legal Doctrine: Immunity of International Rice Research Institute (IRRI) from local labor dispute proceedings pursuant to headquarters agreement.

CASE 16

DFA vs. NLRC

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 113191, September 18, 1996

Key Legal Doctrine: Immunity of Asian Development Bank; DFA diplomatic certification is conclusive on courts.

CASE 17

Fontanilla vs. Maliaman

CITATION Citation: 194 SCRA 486 (1991)

Key Legal Doctrine: Immunity of incorporated government agencies; National Irrigation Administration (NIA) liable for torts when performing proprietary functions.

CASE 18

Farolan vs. CTA

CITATION Citation: 217 SCRA 298 (1993)

Key Legal Doctrine: Unincorporated government agencies performing purely governmental functions (Bureau of Customs) enjoy state immunity from damage suits.

CASE 19

China National Machinery & Equipment Corp. vs. Santamaria

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 185572, February 7, 2012

Key Legal Doctrine: Commercial nature of Northrail contract excludes foreign government corporation from claiming sovereign immunity.

CASE 20

Civil Aeronautics Administration vs. CA

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. L-51806, November 8, 1968

Key Legal Doctrine: Operation of international airport is a proprietary function; CAA is suable for torts and damages resulting from airport maintenance.

CASE 21

Meritt vs. Government of the Philippine Islands

CITATION Citation: 34 Phil. 311, March 21, 1916

Key Legal Doctrine: Classic doctrine that statutory consent to be sued (suability) is not an admission of liability (liability).

CASE 22

Republic vs. Feliciano

CITATION Citation: 148 SCRA 424 (1987)

Key Legal Doctrine: Doctrine of non-suability of the State; necessity of express statutory authority to sue the Republic.

CASE 23

UP vs. Dizon

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 171182, August 23, 2012

Key Legal Doctrine: Procedure for money claims against state universities; exclusive primary jurisdiction of the Commission on Audit (COA) under PD 1445.

CASE 24

City of Manila vs. Teotico

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. L-23052, January 29, 1968

Key Legal Doctrine: Liability of local governments for defective public works and streets under Article 2189 of the Civil Code as a special law.

CASE 25

USA vs. Ruiz

CITATION Citation: 136 SCRA 487 (1985)

Key Legal Doctrine: Restrictive doctrine of state immunity; commercial contract for naval base wharf repair is proprietary in character.

CASE 26

Froilan vs. Pan Oriental Shipping

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. L-6060, September 30, 1950

Key Legal Doctrine: Implied waiver of state immunity when the government files a complaint against a private party, opening itself to counterclaims.

CASE 27

Municipality of San Miguel vs. Fernandez

CITATION Citation: 130 SCRA 56 (1984)

Key Legal Doctrine: Consent to be sued does not include consent to execution; municipal and public funds are exempt from execution and garnishment.

CASE 28

Municipality of Makati vs. CA

CITATION Citation: 190 SCRA 206 (1990)

Key Legal Doctrine: Garnishment of municipal funds is prohibited without prior legislative appropriation ordinance.

CASE 29

Wylie vs. Rarang

CITATION Citation: 209 SCRA 357 (1992)

Key Legal Doctrine: State immunity cannot be used as a shield to perpetrate an injustice; public officers are personally liable for ultra vires and defamatory acts.

Topic III: The Fundamental Powers of the State

Subject Scope: Police Power, Eminent Domain (Just Compensation), and Taxation • 25 Assigned Cases

CASE 30

MMDA vs. Bel-Air Village Association, Inc.

CITATION Citation: 328 SCRA 836, March 27, 2000

Key Legal Doctrine: Police power is inherently legislative; MMDA is an administrative coordinator and lacks delegated police power or ordinance-making powers.

CASE 31

PASEI vs. Drilon

CITATION Citation: 163 SCRA 386 (1988)

Key Legal Doctrine: Valid exercise of police power in temporarily suspending the deployment of female domestic workers abroad for welfare protection.

CASE 32

Tio vs. Videogram Regulatory Board

CITATION Citation: 151 SCRA 208 (1987)

Key Legal Doctrine: Interplay of police power and regulatory taxation over the videogram industry to curb piracy and promote public morals.

CASE 33

Acebedo Optical Company, Inc. vs. CA

CITATION Citation: 329 SCRA 314 (2000)

Key Legal Doctrine: Delegation of police power to city mayors; mayor cannot impose conditions beyond statutory professional regulations in issuing permits.

CASE 34

Taxicab Operators of MM vs. BOT

CITATION Citation: 117 SCRA 597 (1982)

Key Legal Doctrine: Phasing out of taxicabs older than 6 years in Metro Manila is a valid exercise of police power for commuter safety and pollution control.

CASE 35

Del Mar vs. PAGCOR

CITATION Citation: 346 SCRA 485 (2000)

Key Legal Doctrine: PAGCOR charter does not grant authority to operate or franchise jai-alai betting operations through private joint ventures.

CASE 36

Angeles University Foundation vs. City of Angeles

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 189999, June 27, 2012

Key Legal Doctrine: Building permit fees are regulatory police power impositions, not taxes; non-stock educational institutions are not exempt from permit fees.

CASE 37

Southern Luzon Drug Corporation vs. DSWD

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 199669, April 25, 2017

Key Legal Doctrine: The 20% Senior Citizen and PWD discount is a valid exercise of Police Power, not Eminent Domain; tax deduction is sufficient.

CASE 38

Republic vs. Salem Investment Corporation

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 137569, June 23, 2000

Key Legal Doctrine: Requisites for valid eminent domain; necessity of establishing a genuine public use before private property is taken.

CASE 39

Estate of Salud Jimenez vs. PEZA

CITATION Citation: 349 SCRA 240, January 16, 2001

Key Legal Doctrine: Abandonment of public purpose in expropriation entitles previous landowner to reacquire the expropriated property.

CASE 40

MCIAA vs. Lozada, Jr.

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 176625, February 25, 2010

Key Legal Doctrine: Right of former owners to repurchase expropriated airport lot upon abandonment of the airport expansion project.

CASE 41

Republic vs. Lim

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 161656, June 29, 2005

Key Legal Doctrine: Non-payment of just compensation within 5 years from finality of expropriation judgment entitles owner to recover possession.

CASE 42

Heirs of Alberto Suguitan vs. City of Mandaluyong

CITATION Citation: 328 SCRA 137, March 14, 2000

Key Legal Doctrine: LGU expropriation under Section 19 of the Local Government Code requires an Ordinance enacted by the Sanggunian, not a mere resolution.

CASE 43

SMI Development Corporation vs. Republic

CITATION Citation: 323 SCRA 862, January 28, 2000

Key Legal Doctrine: Definiteness of public use in eminent domain; expropriation must serve a clearly specified public purpose.

CASE 44

Philippine Press Institute vs. COMELEC

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 119694, May 22, 1995

Key Legal Doctrine: Compelling print media to provide free 'Comelec Space' constitutes taking of private property without just compensation.

CASE 45

TELEBAP, Inc. vs. COMELEC

CITATION Citation: 289 SCRA 337, April 21, 1998

Key Legal Doctrine: Requirement of free broadcast airtime for Comelec is a valid condition of public airwave franchises under police power.

CASE 46

Heirs of Juancho Ardona vs. Reyes

CITATION Citation: 125 SCRA 220 (1983)

Key Legal Doctrine: Concept of public use in eminent domain encompasses tourism development, economic infrastructure, and social promotion.

CASE 47

OSG vs. Ayala Land Incorporated

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 177056, September 18, 2009

Key Legal Doctrine: Prohibiting shopping malls from charging parking fees is an unconstitutional taking of private property without compensation.

CASE 48

Mosqueda vs. Pilipino Banana Growers & Exporters

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 189185, August 16, 2016

Key Legal Doctrine: City ordinance banning aerial spraying is an unreasonable and oppressive police power measure violating due process and equal protection.

CASE 49

Secretary of DPWH vs. Spouses Tecson

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 179334, April 21, 2015

Key Legal Doctrine: Determination of just compensation is based on the fair market value at the time of taking, plus legal interest until fully paid.

CASE 50

NPC vs. Heirs of Macabangkit Sangkay

CITATION Citation: 656 SCRA 60 (2011)

Key Legal Doctrine: Inverse condemnation; construction of underground hydroelectric tunnel without compensation is a taking; action does not prescribe.

CASE 51

EPZA (now PEZA) vs. Pulido

CITATION Citation: 656 SCRA 315 (2011)

Key Legal Doctrine: Application of the Doctrine of the Law of the Case in determining just compensation valuation.

CASE 52

Republic vs. Judge Mupas and PIATCO

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 181892, September 8, 2015

Key Legal Doctrine: Valuation methodology in NAIA Terminal 3 expropriation; replacement cost method and depreciated structural value.

CASE 53

LBP vs. Dalauta

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 190004, August 8, 2017

Key Legal Doctrine: Judicial determination of just compensation is an exclusive court power; DARAB administrative formulas serve as non-binding guidelines.

CASE 54

Alfonso vs. LBP and DAR

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 181912, November 29, 2016

Key Legal Doctrine: Trial courts must consider statutory factors in RA 6657 and DAR formulas in judicially computing agrarian just compensation.

Topic IV: State Policies, Principles and General Considerations

Subject Scope: Republicanism, Separation of Powers, Ecology, Nationalist Provisions, Regalian Doctrine • 11 Assigned Cases

CASE 55

MMDA vs. Concerned Residents of Manila Bay

CITATION Citation: G.R. Nos. 171947-48, February 15, 2011

Key Legal Doctrine: Continuing Mandamus; separation of powers does not bar the Supreme Court from directing executive agencies to perform ministerial duties to clean Manila Bay.

CASE 56

Belgica vs. Ochoa, Jr.

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 208566, November 19, 2013

Key Legal Doctrine: Unconstitutionality of the PDAF / Pork Barrel System; violation of separation of powers and non-delegation doctrine; Two Fundamental Tests of delegation.

CASE 57

Gonzales III vs. Office of the President

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 196231, January 28, 2014

Key Legal Doctrine: Constitutional independence of the Office of the Ombudsman; President cannot administratively discipline the Deputy Ombudsman.

CASE 58

Garcia vs. Executive Secretary

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 198554, July 30, 2012

Key Legal Doctrine: Civilian supremacy over the military (Art. II, Sec. 3); President's power to create fact-finding and truth commissions.

CASE 59

Oposa vs. Factoran, Jr.

CITATION Citation: 224 SCRA 792 (1993)

Key Legal Doctrine: Intergenerational responsibility; the right to a balanced and healthful ecology (Art. II, Sec. 16) is self-executing and confers legal standing on minors.

CASE 60

Cadet 1CL Cudia vs. Superintendent of PMA

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 211362, February 24, 2015

Key Legal Doctrine: Academic freedom of military institutions includes the right to dismiss cadets who violate the Honor Code, provided minimum due process is observed.

CASE 61

Behest Loans Committee vs. Desierto

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 130140, October 25, 1999

Key Legal Doctrine: State right to recover ill-gotten wealth under Art. XI, Sec. 15; prescription for behest loans starts from discovery by investigative committee.

CASE 62

Isagani Cruz vs. Secretary of DENR

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 135385, December 6, 2000

Key Legal Doctrine: Regalian Doctrine (Art. XII, Sec. 2) vs Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act (IPRA / RA 8371); recognition of ancestral domain ownership.

CASE 63

Resident Marine Mammals vs. Sec. Angelo Reyes

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 180771, April 21, 2015

Key Legal Doctrine: Legal standing of human stewards on behalf of cetacean species; unconstitutionality of Service Contract No. 46 in Tañon Strait protected seascape.

CASE 64

Roy III vs. Chairperson Herbosa

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 207246, November 22, 2016

Key Legal Doctrine: The 60-40 Filipino ownership rule for public utilities (Art. XII, Sec. 11) applies separately to both voting shares and beneficial ownership.

CASE 65

PNOC vs. Keppel Philippines Holdings, Inc.

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 202050, July 25, 2016

Key Legal Doctrine: Constitutional prohibition against foreign acquisition of private lands (Art. XII, Sec. 7); option contracts granting land purchase to foreign corporations are void.

Topic V: Legislative Department

Subject Scope: Bicameral Congress, Privileges, Disqualifications, Inquiries, Impeachment, and Legislative Veto • 27 Assigned Cases

CASE 66

Lagman vs. Executive Secretary

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 231658, July 4, 2017

Key Legal Doctrine: Delegated emergency powers; martial law proclamation in Mindanao; separate vs joint review powers of Congress and the Supreme Court.

CASE 67

Trillanes IV vs. Hon. Oscar Pimentel, Sr.

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 179817, June 27, 2008

Key Legal Doctrine: Election to the Senate does not confer immunity from criminal detention for coup d'état; legislative work cannot be performed inside prison.

CASE 68

Aquino vs. COMELEC

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 189793, April 7, 2010

Key Legal Doctrine: Constitutional apportionment of legislative districts; the 250,000 population requirement applies to cities, not to whole provinces.

CASE 69

Bagabuyo vs. COMELEC

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 176970, December 8, 2008

Key Legal Doctrine: Reapportionment of legislative districts via statute does not require a plebiscite under Article X, Section 10.

CASE 70

Sema vs. COMELEC

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 177597, July 16, 2008

Key Legal Doctrine: Power to create legislative districts belongs exclusively to the national Congress; ARMM regional assembly cannot create provinces or districts.

CASE 71

People vs. Jalosjos

CITATION Citation: 324 SCRA 689, February 3, 2000

Key Legal Doctrine: Parliamentary immunity from arrest (Art. VI, Sec. 11) is unavailable for offenses punishable by more than 6 years imprisonment.

CASE 72

Osmeña vs. Pendatun

CITATION Citation: 109 Phil. 863 (1960)

Key Legal Doctrine: Speech and debate clause protects legislators from external liability, but the House retains inherent disciplinary power to suspend its members.

CASE 73

Pobre vs. Santiago

CITATION Citation: A.C. No. 7399, August 25, 2009

Key Legal Doctrine: Absolute parliamentary immunity of Senators for speeches delivered on the Senate floor bars disciplinary disbarment proceedings.

CASE 74

Liban vs. Gordon

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 175352, July 15, 2009 / Jan 18, 2011

Key Legal Doctrine: Incompatible offices under Art. VI, Sec. 13; holding the office of Philippine Red Cross Chairman does not forfeit Senate seat.

CASE 75

Baguilat vs. Speaker Alvarez

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 227757, July 25, 2017

Key Legal Doctrine: Internal election of House officers and recognition of Minority Leader is a non-justiciable political question.

CASE 76

Pimentel vs. HRET

CITATION Citation: 393 SCRA 227 (2002)

Key Legal Doctrine: Independence and constitutional composition of the House of Representatives Electoral Tribunal (HRET).

CASE 77

Vilando vs. HRET

CITATION Citation: 656 SCRA 17 (2011)

Key Legal Doctrine: Exclusive jurisdiction of HRET over election, returns, and qualifications of House members.

CASE 78

Reyes vs. COMELEC

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 207264, June 25, 2013

Key Legal Doctrine: Jurisdiction transition from COMELEC to HRET requires three requisites: valid proclamation, proper oath, and assumption of office.

CASE 79

Velasco vs. Speaker Belmonte

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 211140, January 12, 2016

Key Legal Doctrine: Ministerial duty of the House Speaker to administer oath to the candidate conclusively declared winner by final Supreme Court judgment.

CASE 80

Abayon vs. COMELEC

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 189434, February 11, 2010

Key Legal Doctrine: HRET has exclusive jurisdiction over qualifications and election disputes involving Party-List representatives once proclaimed and seated.

CASE 81

ABAKADA Guro vs. Purisima

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 166715, August 14, 2008

Key Legal Doctrine: Three kinds of legislative oversight: scrutiny, investigation, and supervision; unconstitutionality of legislative veto post-enactment.

CASE 82

Garcilliano vs. House of Representatives

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 170338, December 23, 2008

Key Legal Doctrine: Power of legislative inquiry (Art. VI, Sec. 21) requires publication of inquiry rules of procedure in newspapers of general circulation or Official Gazette.

CASE 83

Senate of the Philippines vs. Ermita

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 169777, April 20, 2006

Key Legal Doctrine: Unconstitutionality of EO 464; Executive Privilege cannot be invoked as an blanket ban preventing cabinet officials from attending inquiries.

CASE 84

Neri vs. Senate

CITATION Citation: G.R. No. 180643, September 5, 2008

Key Legal Doctrine: Elements of Presidential Communications Privilege: relation to quintessential presidential power, authored by close advisors, valid claim.

CASE 85
