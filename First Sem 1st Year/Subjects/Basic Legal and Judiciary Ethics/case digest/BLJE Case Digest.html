CASE 13: Re: Lorenzo "Larry" Gadon

A.C. No. 13521, June 27, 2023 | Per Curiam (En Banc)

I. FACTS OF THE CASE:

Atty. Lorenzo Gadon was captured on a viral video clip hurling extremely foul, misogynistic, and abusive vulgarities

against journalist Raissa Robles. The Supreme Court motu proprio issued a show-cause order and placed him under

preventive suspension. The Court evaluated whether his public conduct and repeated profane outbursts over

social media violated his ethical duties as an officer of the court.

II. STATEMENT OF THE ETHICAL ISSUE:

Whether Atty. Gadon's scandalous, profane, and misogynistic video tirade against a journalist warrants his

disbarment under the CPRA.

III. THE COURT'S RULING (ALAC METHOD):

ANSWER: YES. The Supreme Court unanimously disbarred Atty. Gadon for gross misconduct and repeated

violations of the ethical duties of propriety and dignity.

LEGAL BASIS: CPRA Canon II (Propriety), Sections 1, 2, 13, and 14; Canon VI (Accountability), Sections 33 and 34.

The CPRA specifically regulates social media conduct of lawyers, mandating that lawyers shall not post false,

abusive, scandalous, or degrading content.

APPLICATION / ANALYSIS: The Court held that the legal profession demands high standards of decency, propriety,

and respect. Gadon's vulgar, misogynistic, and abhorrent language displayed in the public sphere over digital

media caused irreparable damage to the public trust and the nobility of the legal profession. His history of previous

suspensions demonstrated an incorrigible disregard for judicial authority and professional decorum.

CONCLUSION / DISPOSITIVE RULING: Atty. Lorenzo Gadon was DISBARRED from the practice of law and his name

stricken from the Roll of Attorneys.

CASE 14: Ventura v. Atty. Samson

A.C. No. 9608, November 27, 2012 | 686 SCRA 430

I. FACTS OF THE CASE:

Complainant engaged the services of respondent Atty. Samson to handle a civil case. The respondent demanded

and received substantial professional fees and filing expenses. However, he failed to file the necessary complaint,

failed to inform the client of the status of the case, and when demanded to return the case funds, refused to

refund the unused amounts, giving evasive excuses.

II. STATEMENT OF THE ETHICAL ISSUE:

Whether Atty. Samson violated the ethical duties of competence, diligence, and fidelity in handling client funds

and causes.

III. THE COURT'S RULING (ALAC METHOD):

ANSWER: YES. Respondent committed gross negligence and dishonesty in handling client funds.

LEGAL BASIS: CPRA Canon III (Fidelity), Sections 1, 2, 6, 7; Canon IV (Competence and Diligence), Sections 1, 2, 3;

CPR Canons 16, 17, 18.

APPLICATION / ANALYSIS: A lawyer owes fidelity to the cause of his client and must be mindful of the trust

reposed in him. When a lawyer receives money from a client for a specific purpose (such as filing fees and

acceptance fee), he holds it in trust and must immediately account for or return it if the purpose is not

accomplished. Failure to render the required legal service and misappropriation of funds constitutes grave ethical

misconduct.

CONCLUSION / DISPOSITIVE RULING: Atty. Samson was SUSPENDED from the practice of law for one (1) year with

an order to immediately RESTITUTE the full amount to the complainant.

Complainant Joselano Guevarra filed a disbarment complaint against Atty. Jose Emmanuel 'Noli' Eala for grossly

wife, Irene Moje, flaunted their relationship in public social events, lived together with her, and sired a child with

her.

II. STATEMENT OF THE ETHICAL ISSUE:

III. THE COURT'S RULING (ALAC METHOD):

ANSWER: YES. Adultery and concubinage by a member of the Bar constitute gross immorality.

reprehensible to a high degree. Siring a child with a married woman while being married himself shows a flagrant

disregard for the sanctity of marriage and the moral integrity required of lawyers.

grossly immoral conduct.

CASE 16: Magayanes v. Vasquez

A.M. No. MTJ-23-014, April 11, 2024

I. FACTS OF THE CASE:

A verified administrative complaint was lodged against a presiding judge for gross misconduct, undue delay in

resolving motions, and inappropriate fraternization with party litigants. The complainant established that the judge

had off-the-record communications with an opposing counsel and accepted hospitality during the pendency of a

disputed land matter.

II. STATEMENT OF THE ETHICAL ISSUE:

Whether the judge violated the New Code of Judicial Conduct and CPRA standards on judicial propriety and

impartiality.

III. THE COURT'S RULING (ALAC METHOD):

ANSWER: YES. The judge committed gross misconduct and violated the mandate of judicial impartiality.

LEGAL BASIS: New Code of Judicial Conduct (A.M. No. 03-05-01-SC), Canons 1 (Independence), 2 (Integrity), 3

(Impartiality), and 4 (Propriety); CPRA Canon II.

APPLICATION / ANALYSIS: Judges must not only be impartial; they must appear impartial at all times. Engaging in

private, ex parte communications and fraternizing with litigants whose cases are pending before their sala destroys

public faith in the integrity of the judicial system. Judges must avoid any appearance of impropriety in all their

activities.

CONCLUSION / DISPOSITIVE RULING: The respondent judge was found GUILTY of gross misconduct and FINED,

with a stern warning against repetition of similar acts.

IV. APPLICABLE CPRA CANONS & ETHICAL PRINCIPLES:

Ethical Synthesis & Canon Application: Under the Code of Professional Responsibility and Accountability (CPRA)

and prevailing jurisprudence, this case squarely governs and applies the principles of NCJC Canon 3: Impartiality,

NCJC Canon 4 / CPRA Canon II: Propriety. Specifically, under NCJC Canon 3: Impartiality, a lawyer or judicial officer

is bound by the mandate that Prohibition against ex parte communications and fraternization with party litigants.

Specifically, under NCJC Canon 4 / CPRA Canon II: Propriety, a lawyer or judicial officer is bound by the mandate

that Duty to preserve the dignity of the bench and avoid all appearances of judicial bias. The Supreme Court ruled

that any deviation from these standards erodes public confidence in the administration of justice and warrants

disciplinary liability, holding that The respondent judge was found GUILTY of gross misconduct and FINED, with a

stern warning against repetition of similar acts.

CASE 17: Linsangan v. Atty. Nicomedes Tolentino

A.C. No. 6672, September 4, 2009 | 598 SCRA 133

I. FACTS OF THE CASE:

Atty. Nicomedes Tolentino operated a law practice where he authorized paralegals and non-lawyer agents (labour

'runners') to solicit seafarers with maritime money claims. He distributed calling cards promising financial loans,

payment of hospital bills, and 30% commission rates, effectively ambulance-chasing overseas workers.

II. STATEMENT OF THE ETHICAL ISSUE:

Whether ambulance-chasing, soliciting legal business through non-lawyer agents, and advancing personal loans to

secure retainers violate legal ethics.

III. THE COURT'S RULING (ALAC METHOD):

ANSWER: YES. Ambulance-chasing and commercialized solicitation of clients are strictly prohibited.

LEGAL BASIS: CPRA Canon II (Propriety), Sections 17 & 18; CPR Canon 2, Rules 2.03 and 3.01; Rule 138, Section 27.

APPLICATION / ANALYSIS: The practice of law is a noble profession and a public calling, not a commercial trade or

moneymaking venture. Lawyers are prohibited from using intermediaries, runners, or commercial advertisements

to recruit clients. Promising monetary loans or financial assistance to prospective clients to capture their cases

constitutes unethical champerty and ambulance-chasing.

CONCLUSION / DISPOSITIVE RULING: Atty. Nicomedes Tolentino was SUSPENDED from the practice of law for one

(1) year.

CASE 76: Venzon v. Peleo III

I. FACTS OF THE CASE:

Complainant filed a disbarment complaint against respondent Atty. Peleo III for chronic refusal to pay lawful just

II. STATEMENT OF THE ETHICAL ISSUE:

III. THE COURT'S RULING (ALAC METHOD):

ANSWER: YES. Issuing bouncing checks and willfully defying lawful debt obligations demonstrate gross dishonesty

LEGAL BASIS: Batas Pambansa Blg. 22; CPRA Canon II (Propriety), Section 1 & 2; CPR Canon 1, Rule 1.01; Section

27, Rule 138.

APPLICATION / ANALYSIS: A lawyer must not engage in unlawful or deceitful conduct. The issuance of bouncing

checks is a crime involving deceit and moral turpitude; it causes injury to public order and commercial trust. A

member of the Bar who repeatedly issues unfunded checks and refuses to settle just debts degrades the honor of

the legal profession.

CONCLUSION / DISPOSITIVE RULING: Atty. Peleo III was SUSPENDED from the practice of law for two (2) years

with a final warning that repetition will result in disbarment.
